This file specifies Layers 2 and 3 of the AC/DC stack: the compilation of a
.fspy document into a debate presentation, and the labelling of that
presentation by argumentation semantics. It supersedes the propagation tables
in [[file:labeller-spec.org]]. Layer 4 (label-guided evaluation) is specified in
[[file:call-by-onus.org]], section "Layer 4".

* Layer architecture

The stack separates five layers. Conflating them (in particular 2 and the
semantic side of 1) was the main defect of the earlier drafts.

- Layer 0 :: the AC/DC *equational theory*: the reductions of the calculus
  that count as equations (bureaucratic/pure expansions, theta-expansion,
  eta for mu binders) versus mere strategy steps (critical-pair
  resolutions). Owner: reduction-spec.org.
- Layer 1 :: the *base category* C: the classifying category of the
  delegation-free fragment, terms modulo the Layer-0 theory under a fixed
  uniform strategy. Target theorem: the CBN quotient is a control category,
  the CBV quotient a co-control category (via the Matilda <-> lambda-mu
  translations).
- Layer 2 :: the *document presentation*: a poly-quiver with scope
  annotations compiled from a .fspy file (this file, section 2). Its
  generated category is the polynomial extension C[X], one indeterminate per
  obligation and presumption. The presentation is syntax; C[X] is semantics;
  the two must never be identified.
- Layer 3 :: the *labelling*: an ADF (abstract dialectical framework)
  compiled from the presentation; strict layer first, then grounded /
  preferred / stable labellings (this file, section 3).
- Layer 4 :: *evaluation*: a witness labelling induces a polarity
  assignment; label-guided reduction is focused reduction under that
  polarization (call-by-onus.org).

* Layer 2: the debate presentation

** Nodes

A node is a proposition. Node identity is the parsed Prop AST modulo
alpha-equivalence of bound first-order variables - never the printed string.
(Prerequisite: threading Prop objects onto AST nodes, tracked as
=aida-first-order-reduction= in tasks.org.) Each node carries a polarity
pair: it can appear on the term side (as a conclusion to prove) and on the
context side (as a claim to refute); these are the same node, not two nodes.

Design decision (recorded, deliberate): one node per proposition collapses
argument identity - attack and support are claim-centric, targeting every
use of the proposition. Attacking one argument's use of A but not another's
is not expressible. This matches the universal-substitution reading of
rational closure.

** Hyperedges

One node per proposition and one *binary* edge per subargument is too
coarse: an argument with several premises is not single-source. A
subargument in theta-normal form exposes exactly one open site per
logically-progressing (non-bureaucratic) AST node; those sites are its
sources.

A *hyperedge* e consists of:

- =term(e)= :: the argument term (a linear or dual-linear AC expression in
  obligation-eta long form, cf. constructing_enthymemes Def. of arguments);
- =target(e)= :: the conclusion node, with side (term/context);
- =sources(e)= :: the finite multiset of open sites of term(e): its
  obligations (Goal/Laog), presumptions (Deleg/Geled), and free assumption
  variables, each a (node, side, kind) triple with
  kind in {obligation, presumption, assumption};
- =scope(e)= :: the binder-scope annotation: for every source site, the list
  of propositions mu/mu'-bound on the spine between the root of term(e) and
  that site, with binding side. This is the VARS data of labeller-spec.org,
  made part of the graph. A bare proposition multigraph cannot detect
  capture; scope(e) is what makes question-begging capture (section 2.4)
  decidable on the graph.
- =strict(e)= :: true iff sources(e) contains no presumption and no
  obligation, i.e. the derivation uses no indeterminates. Strictness is a
  property of edges, never a truth value of nodes.

The presentation of a .fspy document is the poly-quiver of all nodes and
hyperedges of its registered arguments, attacks and supports, closed under
subarguments (every subargument of a registered argument contributes its own
hyperedge).

** Why hyperedges and not product objects

Categorical semantics normally absorbs multi-source morphisms into product
objects. That is exactly what Layer 1 does; the hyperedges are the Layer-2
presentation of the same data before representation.

- Presentation vs representation. A hyperedge is a multicategory /
  polycategory arrow; "use products instead" is the statement that the
  multicategory is representable. The free representable multicategory on a
  multigraph is (equivalent to) the free monoidal category, so the two
  encodings generate equivalent categories. In the model, products absorb
  the sources on the domain and the par-like connective absorbs the
  succedent on the codomain: a judgment Gamma |- sigma -| Delta denotes a
  morphism [Gamma] -> [sigma] par [Delta]. The presentation defers both
  representations, for two reasons: the object fragment has no
  conjunction/product syntax (product nodes would be propositions nobody
  asserted, attacked or supported), and under Layer-4 polarization "the"
  product splits into positive tensor vs negative with (CBV tuples values,
  CBN projects from lazy pairs) - bunching premises at Layer 2 would commit
  a polarity before the labelling decides it.
- Argumentation-layer reasons. Attack, support and rational closure target
  propositions; premise sharing across arguments must be visible as
  node-sharing (a PxQ node hides the shared P); and a product node's only
  sensible label is the meet of its factors, which is precisely the
  conjunction-over-sources acceptance condition the hyperedge already
  carries.
- The stoup reading. The dialectical ND transposition of the calculus
  (COMMA paper, after Herbelin / Lovas-Crary / Harper) keeps the succedent:
  judgments are Sigma |- v:sigma -| Delta with a single active stoup
  formula, not single-conclusion ND. The transposition preserves
  derivability and the equational theory, so the classifying category is
  unchanged up to equivalence. A hyperedge is exactly a stoup judgment, in
  the following sense: target(e) is the stoup formula with its side, and
  sources(e) is Sigma ⊎ Delta of the *variable-ified* argument - name each
  open site (obligation/presumption) with a fresh variable of its type and
  side and the result is a plain term whose sequent
  Sigma' |- v : sigma -| Delta' carries exactly sources(e) on its two
  sides, with the kind-tags remembering only the burden of proof
  (assumption / obligation / presumption). The role of theta-normal form:
  the root sequent exists for any term, but the sequents of the
  *subarguments* live in the typing derivation, which is meta-level and
  invisible in the term. Theta-expansion reifies that derivation into the
  syntax - every logically-progressing node's local sequent becomes surface
  structure via its exposed obligation - so the compiler reads one
  hyperedge per subargument off the term mechanically, without
  reconstructing the derivation.
  The stoup is a focus (LKT/LKQ disciplines ~ CBN/CBV), which is the bridge
  to Layer-4 polarization, and the stoup discipline is what tracks the
  centrality restrictions of the premonoidal par syntactically.

** Semantics: indeterminates, not morphisms

The generated category of the presentation is the polynomial extension
C[X] of the base category C, with one freely adjoined indeterminate per
obligation and per presumption:

- a term-side site of type sigma adjoins x : T -> sigma;
- a context-side site adjoins the dual x' : sigma -> F.

Presumptions and obligations are NOT definable morphisms of C. Adjoining a
genuine global element T -> sigma would make sigma semantically true,
collapse strict and defeasible, and destroy direct consistency. As
indeterminates:

- grafting = substitution: filling a site is the universal property of
  C[X] (a functor C[X] -> C[X'] choosing a morphism for x);
- instantiation never happens inside C[X]: indeterminates stay
  indeterminate there, and that IS defeasibility. Instantiation is an
  *evaluation functor out of* C[X]: by the universal property, each choice
  of a morphism m for x induces ev_m : C[X] -> C[X\{x}]. These functors
  read off the computational content of an *outcome* after Layers 3-4 have
  run; they are not operations on the debate. Three cases:
  - defeat (attacker IN): the site is NOT instantiated. What reduction
    leaves there is the clash itself,

    :   mu alpha:A.< t || E >        (alpha unused)

    the paper's abort (COMMA, Definition Attack: the steal wing
    < x || t2 > with x bound to the original): the site's original t -
    its derivation, or its bare indeterminate, the presumption that was
    refuted or the open obligation - facing the winning refutation E
    under an affine binder. The site stays an indeterminate wherever t
    is one; the clash records that its continuation has been taken by E.
    (Revised 2026-09-17: an earlier version of this bullet, written
    against the shapes the term-level verbs graft, had =mu _.<[:A] || E>=
    with a fresh obligation in place of t; that is the special case in
    which the original was a bare obligation.) Semantically this is *not* an
    instantiation of x at all: it is composition with the refutation, in
    C[X], with x still free. (An earlier draft of this bullet called it
    "ev at the abort morphism T -> F -> A, the vacuous affine witness of
    main.tex:300". That misread the author's ednote, which is ex
    contradictione quodlibet - mu_:A.<F || F> witnesses A *given a
    contradiction already in hand* - and has nothing to do with a defeated
    site, which holds a refutation facing a hole, not a contradiction.)
    Operationally nothing is grafted at evaluation time: the attack
    scaffold was built by graft at construction time, and reduction under
    the sigma-polarized strategy leaves this throw at the site, so any
    continuation demanding the site consumes an exception;
  - acceptance by derivation (a supporter d for A is IN): ev at the
    denotation of d - syntactically, grafting the supporter;
  - IN by default (unattacked, unsupported presumption): NO instantiation.
    The normal form retains the indeterminate; the debate's value is
    polynomial in x, a proof modulo the presumption. This matches
    delegations surviving in AIDA normal forms today. Retraction or update
    on new arguments = composing with a different evaluation functor;
  The three cases assemble into the statement of adequacy (T3), with one
  correction to its earlier form: only the *acceptance-by-derivation*
  case instantiates an indeterminate. Let m(sigma) send each site
  accepted by derivation to its supporter's denotation and leave every
  other site free. Then reduction under the sigma-polarized strategy
  computes ev_m(sigma) composed with the defeat throws:
  [[NF_sigma(d)]] = ev_m(sigma)([[d]]) with, at each site sigma defeats,
  the clash mu alpha.< t || E > in place of the original t - up to
  the Layer-0 equational theory (undemanded sites are affinely discarded,
  and affine discard is an equation). Both sides of the square therefore
  live in C[X], not in C: a defeated debate is still polynomial in its
  defeated sites, and the OUT row of the square is checked by
  substituting the *clash* for the site by hand, never a closed term.
  The OUT-side promise that follows is "not a value" (an obligation or
  an uncatchable clash remains; the classifier reports the latter as an
  exception wherever it sits); "OUT implies a closed exception at the
  root" is a separate claim requiring abort propagation through axiom-headed spines
  (task aida-abort-propagation), which the calculus does not currently
  provide and whose soundness is unexamined;
- obligation vs presumption is not a semantic distinction. Both are
  indeterminates; they differ only in default label (OUT vs IN, Layer 3)
  and burden of proof. The asymmetry lives entirely in the argumentation
  layer;
- conservativity of C[X] over C is the precise statement of "a delegation
  may be assumed until the contrary is proven".

Fragment note: an individual hyperedge - a linear (dual-linear) argument,
i.e. Delta restricted to the single stoup successor - denotes in the
intuitionistic, cartesian closed fragment; only the debate operations
(grafting, capture, attack/support composition) engage the control
structure. Adjoining indeterminates is available in both settings, which is
why the treatment of obligations and presumptions is uniform across the
fragment boundary.

** Canonical naming and question-begging capture

Grafting is deliberately capture-permitting, but capture is name-sensitive:
without a naming discipline, alpha-equivalent rootstocks produce
non-alpha-equivalent debate terms and neither the compilation nor the
semantics is well defined.

Discipline (locally-named): free assumption variables are canonical per
(proposition, side); bound variables are alpha-convertible as usual;
grafting permits capture only at canonical assumption names. FreshenBinderNames
(core/comp/alpha.py) enforces the bound half; the canonical-assumption half
is currently only a convention in examples and MUST become a checked
invariant of Argument construction.

An attack or support graft *begs the question* iff a source site of the
scion's edge names a proposition that occurs, with the appropriate side, in
scope(e) of the rootstock edge at the graft site. The graft then captures:
the composite is the rootstock's argument from contradiction. Begging the
question is thereby a propositional property, independent of naming
accidents, and decidable from the presentation alone (sources x scope).

** Rational closure

Rational closure (attacks/supports propagate to and from subarguments) is
specified as *reachability closure on the presentation*: an edge targeting
node n engages every edge with source or target n, and subargument edges
are already present by construction. It is NEVER performed as term-level
substitution - that is the operation that explodes and need not terminate.
The presentation is finite, so closure is finite. Debate terms are
recovered on demand by *unfolding* (section 4).

Semantic justification of the closure requirement: ev_m substitutes at
*every* occurrence of an indeterminate - functors do not act on single
occurrences of a generator. A partially grafted attack (some sites of the
proposition left unwrapped) reduces to a term that is ev_m of nothing: one
occurrence reflects defeat while another retains the default. Rational
closure is exactly the condition under which evaluation has a functorial
description, i.e. under which adequacy (T3) is even well-posed.

* Layer 3: labelling as an ADF

The graph has support and attack, default-labelled leaves, and a strict
layer. This is not a Dung AF, and bipolar-AF semantics are not assumed.
The structure is an abstract dialectical framework: every node gets an
acceptance condition over its parents.

** Strict layer first

Before any defeasible labelling:

1. compute the strict subgraph (edges with strict(e));
2. close the finite set of present strict hyperedges under *transposition*:
   an n-source strict edge contributes its n re-orientations (swap the
   target with one source, negating sides), so premise-attacks through
   strict edges exist. This is linear in the edge set, decidable, and
   purely syntactic even first-order (the transposal reuses the edge's own
   terms; no unification search). No proof search and no deductive closure
   is performed: the graph never invents strict derivations beyond
   re-orientations of edges the document already contains.
   Two levels must not be confused here:
   - meta-theorem (cited by the T4 consistency proof, never computed): the
     ambient classical sequent calculus is closed under transposition -
     for every strict derivation and premise the transposed derivation
     exists via mu/mu' rebinding (what the contraposition fixtures
     exercise term-level). Had this failed, restricted rebut would have
     been required instead;
   - honest gap, part of T4's content: the closure/consistency postulates
     quantify over the infinite deductive closure of accepted conclusions.
     The proof obligation is that present-edges-plus-transposals already
     generate enough attacks to prevent inconsistent joint acceptance;
     the fixture suite checks finite instances of the known failure modes;
3. detect strict contradictions: a node with strict edges to both its term
   and context side. Mark the *edges involved* CONTR.

STR and CONTR are edge/derivation properties, not additional truth values
of the Layer-3 fixpoint. CONTR propagates only along dependency edges
(reachability in the presentation); an isolated contradictory component
must not affect the labels of nodes not depending on it
(non-interference; see fixture =contamination= in tests/rationality/).

** Acceptance conditions

For each node n, side s, the acceptance condition is compiled from the
incoming edges:

- each supporting edge e with target (n,s) contributes a conjunct-free
  disjunct: the conjunction of the acceptance of its sources
  (support is disjunctive: each undefeated supporter suffices);
- an obligation site contributes the literal =false= by default (accepted
  only if some edge derives it);
- a presumption site contributes =true= by default (rejected only if some
  edge derives its contrary);
- an attacking edge on (n,s) is a supporting edge on the contrary side
  (n, s'); the condition for (n,s) conjoins the negation of the
  acceptance of (n,s');
- that negation is ASYMMETRIC (author, 2026-09-25). A presumption is a
  delegation: it hands the burden of refutation to the other side. So a
  side that carries only a default marker, with no edge deriving it, does
  not conjoin back against a side that is derived - the derivation
  defeats it outright. The conjunction is added to a side's DERIVED
  disjuncts only when the contrary side is itself derived; a
  presumption's own disjunct always carries it. Consequence: an undercut
  of a presumed premise decides the contest instead of leaving it UNDEC,
  and a defeasible argument beats a bare default as decisively as a
  strict one does;
- a default marker on BOTH sides of one proposition is an ONUS CONFLICT:
  each side delegates the burden to the other and neither holds it. It
  should be refused where the presentation is built, which needs the onus
  to be tracked by the prover or the wrapper (task
  aida-onus-delegation-polarity); until then the compiler warns and keeps
  both conjunctions, i.e. the old mutual rebut. Opposing OBLIGATIONS are
  not a conflict: nobody delegated, and both sides stay OUT;
- a strict edge contributes with weight "strict": it can only be
  countered by CONTR, not by defeasible attack.

** Semantics

Labellings are the standard ADF semantics over the three values
IN/OUT/UNDEC:

- grounded :: least fixed point of the three-valued (Kleene) evaluation of
  the acceptance conditions. Unique; this is the default labelling.
- preferred / stable :: as in ADF theory; generally multiple.

The ordering STR < IN < UNDEC < OUT of labeller-spec.org and the
_combine_pair tables of core/comp/color.py are fragments of the Kleene
evaluation and are superseded by it; recasting removes the
ValueError("incomplete") holes.

Compatibility requirement (verification target T5): on tree-shaped
(cycle-free, sharing-free) debates the ADF grounded labelling must agree
with the existing term labeller on the green/red/yellow ~ IN/OUT/UNDEC
correspondence; the term labeller then becomes a derived view.

* Layer 2->4 interface: unfolding

A debate term for an entry point (the issue) is generated by unfolding the
presentation from that node: choose edges per the labelling, expand
subarguments, and break cycles by mu/mu'-capture at the first repeated
(proposition, side) on the current spine.

Obligation (verification target T6): unfolding terminates and is
well-typed. Measure: each (proposition, side) pair may be captured at most
once per branch, so branch length is bounded by 2 * |nodes|.

* Specification obligations (verification targets)

Tracked in tasks.org under "Specification and verification".

- T1 :: sigma-guided reduction is deterministic and terminating on
  compiled debate terms; normalization is stable under grafting
  (labelling commutes with substitution of an argument for an
  indeterminate of equal status). Without stability, composition in the
  generated category is not associative and Layers 1-2 collapse.
- T2 :: Layer-1 quotients satisfy the (co)control-category axioms; C[X] is
  conservative over C; the Layer-4 semantics is a polarized/adjunction
  model with the uniform CBN/CBV strategies as degenerate cases; the
  dualizing functor swaps term/context and credulous/skeptical.
- T3 (flagship) :: computational adequacy, for *founded* witness
  labellings: if the witness labelling sigma labels the issue IN, the
  unfolded debate term normalizes to a value; if OUT, to an
  abort/exception term; if UNDEC, mode-dependent per the polarity policy.
  Equivalently: NF under the sigma-polarized strategy computes
  ev_m(sigma) up to the Layer-0 theory (the commuting square in the
  Semantics subsection of Layer 2). Proof by induction on the fixpoint
  stages of sigma.
  Restricted 2026-10-16 (the author; tasks.org,
  aida-judge-by-what-it-stands-for). A labelling is founded if every
  statement it labels IN is IN by a derivation whose IN sources are
  reached in earlier stages, so that no IN statement rests on itself;
  the grounded labelling always is, a preferred labelling that accepts
  an unfounded loop (P from Q, Q from P, with no other ground) is not.
  For such a labelling ev_m(sigma) is not well-founded - the supporter's
  denotation contains the site it would replace - and evaluation, which
  judges every expression by what it stands for, returns open where the
  label says IN. The label still records that the loop is admissible.
- T4 :: rationality postulates for the induced instantiation: closure,
  direct and indirect consistency, subargument closure,
  non-interference/crash-resistance. Machine side: fixture suite in
  tests/rationality/ with recorded expected labellings.
- T5 :: compilation correctness (agreement with the term labeller on
  trees; regression against the current color.py fixtures).
- T6 :: unfolding totality (section 4).
