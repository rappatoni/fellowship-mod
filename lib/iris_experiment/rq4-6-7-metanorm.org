#+language: en

AI DRAFT (Phases 3-5 of task =aida-iris-usufruct=).

- *What it is:* one example in three layers, all about a single entry:
  - layer 1 (RQ 4): the unwritten meta-norm that obliges the register
    court to enter a fact
  - layer 2 (RQ 6): what the applicant must show and what the court must
    examine
  - layer 3 (RQ 7): the claim to registration and the scope of
    examination, each argued from the other
- *Logic:* first order with reified eventualities (=analysis.org= § 3).
  - =Enter e c f= says that e is an entering of fact f by court c.
  - The deontic notions are ordinary predicates over agents and an
    eventuality: =Duty c a e= (c owes a the eventuality e) and
    =Claim a c e= (a has a claim against c to e).
  - Sources: Hobbs 1985; Robaldo & Sun 2017; Robaldo et al. 2020
    (DAPRECO). Hohfeld's correlativity of claim and duty is written as
    two universal rules, one per direction (Hohfeld 1913; Sergot 2001).
    Full references: =README.org=.
- *The entry:* to keep the burden of proof apart from the RQ 3 dispute,
  the example uses the uncontroversial part of the IRIS scenario.
  - The limited partner B transfers the interest to C, who joins as
    limited partner by special succession (/Sonderrechtsnachfolge/). The
    partners apply for the entry of C.
  - This entry is provided by statute (§§ 162 (1), 106 (2), 107 HGB).
  - For the usufruct itself, =Registrable= is the issue RQ 3 leaves open.
    It would take the place of =ProvidedByStatute= below.
- *How the scenarios are built:* AIDA scripts accumulate arguments, so
  each scenario is its own script. The blocks below are named. Each
  scenario at the end of a section assembles its script from them with
  noweb references and states its expected verdict before the run. The
  recorded verdicts are pinned in =tests/iris/test_iris.py=.
- *Legal content:* the readings of German law below are an AI draft. The
  IRIS draft's §§ 3.1-3.2 are the source. Every reading needs legal
  review.

Figures (=make iris=, in =figures/=): =duty_graph.png= is scenario 6a

* Layer 1 (RQ 4): the gap as a delegation

#+begin_quote
Eine solche geschriebene Meta-Rechtsnorm existiert jedoch nicht.
[...] Die Existenz einer Metanorm wird also von den geschriebenen
Rechtsregeln vorausgesetzt. (IRIS draft, §§ 3.1, 3.2)
#+end_quote

/AI DRAFT TRANSLATION — not reviewed by a lawyer./ Such a written meta-norm
does not exist, however. [...] The existence of a meta-norm is thus
presupposed by the written legal rules.

The meta-norm in the IRIS draft's wording (§ 3.2 (1) sentence 1): "The
register court has to enter a fact in the commercial register if the
formal and substantive requirements for registration are met."

The encoding:
- =MetaNorm= is a statement: "the unwritten meta-norm is in force".
- =bridge= is the meta-norm's content, conditional on it being in force.
  The requirements are folded into one helper statement, =Passed e=
  ("the examination of e is passed"). Layer 2 opens it up.
- =duty= derives the court's duty. Both =MetaNorm= and =Passed e1= are
  *delegations* (=by default.=). Nothing in the written law supplies the
  meta-norm, so the argument hands it on as a presumption. That is the
  gap.

* Layer 2 (RQ 6): what must be shown, and by whom

#+begin_quote
Diese Befugnis wird auf der Beweisebene jedoch dahingehend
eingeschränkt, dass das Gericht grundsätzlich von der Richtigkeit der
mitgeteilten Tatsachen auszugehen hat. Die mitgeteilten
(Rechts-)Tatsachen begrenzen wiederum den normativen Prüfungsumfang –
insbesondere ist eine solche grundsätzlich nur insoweit vorzunehmen, als

(Rechts-)Tatsachen vorsieht. (IRIS draft, § 3.1)
#+end_quote

/AI DRAFT TRANSLATION — not reviewed by a lawyer./ On the level of
evidence, however, this power is restricted: the court has, in
principle, to assume that the reported facts are correct. The reported
(legal) facts in turn limit the normative scope of examination. In
particular, an examination is in principle to be made only so far as the
law also provides for the submission of the (legal) facts required for
it.

"Burden of proof" here means, as the author settled (2026-11-13), the
standard the applicant has to meet and, dually, the depth of the court's
examination. Register proceedings are ex officio proceedings (§ 26
FamFG), so this is not a civil-procedure burden (=README.org=, AI NOTE).

The encoding opens the helper =Passed e1= of layer 1. Each requirement
is a necessary condition, stated as a rule from =Passed= and attacked by
one counterargument. Who carries the burden for a requirement is decided
by one choice inside its counterargument:
- the refutation of the requirement is *presumed* (=by default.=): the
  applicant has to establish the requirement;
- the refutation is *owed* (left open): the court has to find a reason
  against it.

| Requirement                    | Rule    | Refutation | Who must act                              |
|--------------------------------+---------+------------+-------------------------------------------|
| abstract registrability        | =n_reg= | presumed   | the applicant (restraint, cf. RQ 3)       |
| documents the law requires     | =n_doc= | presumed   | the applicant must submit them            |
| correctness of reported facts  | =n_cor= | owed       | the court, by a justified doubt           |

* Layer 2b (RQ 6, "verarbeiten"): the evaluation returns the court's order

RQ 6 asks how the burden of proof can be represented *and processed*.
Layer 2 shows that the debate's outcome depends on who must act. This
layer uses that outcome: once the debate is complete, the evaluation
returns a concrete object, namely the order the court has to make, with
its justification attached.

The orders, as individuals of a sort =Order=:
- =o_entry=: enter C as limited partner (an entry order).
- =o_submit=: an interim order (/Zwischenverfügung/, § 382 (4) FamFG)
  asking for the application in certified form.
- =o_prove=: an interim order asking the applicants to clear the doubt
  the register raises.

When is each order lawful?
- *The entry order* is lawful if the court owes the entry (=entry_rule=,
  from =Duty=; owed).
- *The submission order* is lawful by default, since a missing document
  is a defect the applicants can remedy. It is moot once the application
  is submitted (=submitted_moots=), and unlawful when the court must
  enter (=no_delay_submit=): a complete application may not be delayed.
- *The proof order* is lawful if the register contradicts the reported
  change. The rule is presumed, not strict: a contradiction calls for
  clarification unless it has been resolved. The order is unlawful when
  the court must enter (=no_delay_prove=). (A first draft stated the rule
  strictly; the order then survived the proof in 6d, because a strict
  argument cannot be defeated.)
- =decision= proves that some order is lawful by its own open choice
  =?o=. The debate tries the declared orders as witnesses and keeps one
  that stands (AIDA's witness scaffold, =README.md=, Discourse
  referents).

  established, and so what an appeal could attack.
- *The burden decides the output.* The same document, with one site
  changed from presumed to owed, yields a different order. An interim
  order is the processed form of an unmet obligation of the applicant.
- *The depth of examination, documented.* The certificate's delegations
  are what the court relied on without examining it: the meta-norm, and
  the examination presumed passed once no requirement's refutation
  stands. The face-value presumption does not appear there. It acts in
  the labelling, where it defeats the correctness objection, and that
  scaffold is dropped from the normal form, so a full record would add
  the labelling. That is the dual sense of "burden of proof" the author settled
  (2026-11-13): what the applicant had to show, and how deep the court
  looked. A register court could file the certificate with the entry,
  as a record of its due diligence.
- *An idea, not tested: the evaluation mode as a standard of proof.*
  Skeptical evaluation demands that the outcome hold in every stable
  reading, while credulous evaluation is satisfied by one consistent
  reading. A procedure could prescribe the mode per kind of entry,
  stricter where an entry has constitutive effect.
- *Classification.* =evaluate= classifies the outcome as a value, an
  exception (an uncatchable clash) or open, with the unmet obligations
  named (=?OUT:…=). A register process could dispatch on these classes:
  - value: execute the order
  - open: ask for what is named
  - exception: the documents contradict each other
- *What it does not do (yet).*
  - The orders are individuals the author declares. AIDA tries them as
    candidates and keeps one that stands; it does not compose an order
    from parts.
  - When two candidates stand, the scaffold keeps the topmost one, as at
    any statement. Candidates are stacked in the order in which the
    document graph first holds a statement about them (=_alternatives=
    in =core/dc/unfold.py=): in practice the order of the arguments for
    them, the later on top. The declaration order of the constants plays
    no part.
    - Checked 2026-11-14 with entry and interim order both standing:
      registering =lawful_interim= last kept the interim order, and
      registering it first kept the entry order.
    - This is AIDA's natural preference (the later statement stands
      above), but the README does not say how declared constants are
      ordered.
    - Legally, the choice between two lawful orders needs a rule, not the
      order of the arguments. The encoding states it by making the orders
      exclude each other (=no_delay_*=, =submitted_moots=), so exactly one
      stands in each scenario.
  - The rendering of the evaluated decision (=render decision
    argumentation evaluated=) mixes the English style with the German
    check-list phrases ("Für … ist hinreichend, dass", "weil", "und")
    at the cut. That is a rendering defect, to be recorded as an AIDA
    task.

* Layer 2c (RQ 6): the witness rises to the top

Layer 2b let the argument name its own open choice, and the debate tried
the declared orders. Here the argument does not name the order. It
proves "some order is lawful" in a way that leaves the order to be
determined, and the evaluation brings a concrete witness to the head of
the normal form. Two ways:

- *Constructively, by cut elimination.* =decision_c= opens the court's
  finding "some document the law requires is missing" (=finding=) and
  builds the order =interim d= (an interim order for that document) for
  whatever =d= the finding supplies. It never mentions =d_appl=. When
  the evaluation meets the finding's pair with this elimination, AIDA's
  rule =<(u, v) || (y).e> → <v || e[u/y]>= (=README.md=, First-order
  debates) substitutes the witness, so =interim d_appl= comes to the top.
- *Classically, by an argued Peirce step.* =decision_k= offers the entry
  order first. To show that the entry is owed, it uses Peirce at the
  duty (=peirce_duty=, argued as in =tests/discourse/drinker_closed.fspy=).
  Assuming the entry is not owed, it offers the claim again with the
  submission order, and the claim's own binder catches that second
  offer. The argument never decides whether the entry is owed; the
  debate's labelling decides it, and with it which witness the normal
  form shows.
  - The open site of =peirce_duty=, "the entry is not owed", is
    presumed: a duty that is not established counts as not owed.
  - Owed instead, it would leave 6b open, since "not established" is
    not "refuted". A first run showed exactly that.

AI NOTE (observations for the authors):
- *Constructive.* The order's content is computed. =decision_c= is a
  general rule ("for any missing required document, an interim order for
  it"), and the evaluation instantiates it with what the debate
  established, so the interim order names the document. The witness term
  is a function application, =interim d_appl=, not a declared constant.
- *Classical.* An existence proof that does not decide the case still
  evaluates to a definite witness. The case split on the duty sits in
  the Peirce step. The labelling resolves it, and the normal form keeps
  either the first offer (with the duty's justification substituted) or
  the second (with the presumption it rests on). This is the drinker
  paradox's mechanism, applied to a legal decision.
- *The onus decides how the case split is read.* With the "not owed" site
  presumed, an unestablished duty yields the interim order. Owed, it
  yields no order at all (open). Which reading a register procedure
  wants is a legal choice, and AIDA makes it one tactic.
- *Limits.*
  - The classical variant here has no proof order: the doubt of 6k-c
    yields the general interim order.
  - The renderer fills decorations of predicates and constants, but not
    of function symbols. A template for =interim= was printed with its
    =@arg1= unfilled, and the witness line prints the raw term
    (=witnessed by interim d_appl=). So =interim= is left undecorated.
    The witness and instantiation lines also print decorated constants
    raw (=instantiated at e1=). Recorded on main as
    =aida-render-fo-terms=, reproduced there and with its causes in
    =pres/decorations.py= and =pres/nl.py=.
  - Combining both variants, with the second offer being =interim d= from
    the finding, is a straightforward next step and was not done.

The encoding:
- Correlativity: =corr1= (duty ⇒ claim) and =corr2= (claim ⇒ duty).
- The court demands a document the law does not require (the transfer
  agreement). If the demand is within the scope of examination, its
  absence defeats =Passed e1= (=court_extra=, with =Proper= presumed).
- The BGH line: the claim limits the scope (=limited=), and a limited
  scope makes a demand for an unrequired document improper
  (=bgh_limit=).
- So the claim defends =Passed e1= against the demand, and =Passed e1=
  grounds the duty, which grounds the claim. That is the cycle.

AI NOTE (observations for the authors):
- AIDA represents the circular argument as it is and does not refuse
  it. Under stable semantics the circle supports two readings:
  - the claim exists, so the scope is limited, the demand is improper,
    the examination passes and the duty, hence the claim, follows;
  - there is no claim, so the demand stands, the examination fails and
    there is no duty.

  Grounded semantics leaves all of it undecided. That is the formal
  counterpart of the IRIS draft's observation that duty of examination,
  power of examination and claim were assumed to "always correspond".
- An independent reason for one link decides the circle (7b, 7d). Whether
  that reason is a presumption (the case-law principle of the normative
  scope, 7b) or strict (a statute fixing the scope, as § 38 AktG does for
  a stock corporation, 7d) is visible in the graph: a delegation in one
  case, an axiom in the other. That is the cross-field contrast of
  RQ 7.
- A counterargument on one side of the circle propagates to the other
  (7c). The doubt defeats the examination, and with it the duty and the
  claim, in the only stable labelling. The claim's reading in which it
  defended itself disappears.
