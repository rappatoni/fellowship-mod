** The issue: five stable labellings

=label issue :Registrable[usufruct] stable= finds five stable
labellings. The rows below are the statements that decide them (=t= is
the statement, =c= its refutation):

| Statement                  | [1] | [2] | [3] | [4] | [5] |
|----------------------------+-----+-----+-----+-----+-----|
| Registrable [t]            | OUT | IN  | IN  | IN  | IN  |
| Registrable [c]            | IN  | OUT | OUT | OUT | OUT |
| CoDetermines               | OUT | IN  | IN  | IN  | IN  |
| Powerless                  | IN  | IN  | IN  | OUT | OUT |
| ConsentConstitutive        | OUT | OUT | OUT | IN  | IN  |
| LikeExecutorship           | OUT | OUT | IN  | OUT | IN  |

The same in all five:
- OUT: =VotesDayToDay=, =ManagementShare=, =Liability=, =InfoSuffices=,
  =DelayDefeatsInterest=, =LiabilityOnly=.
- IN: =Unlike=, =ClarityPrecedence=.

Reading of the table:
- Labelling [1] is the position of München and Köln. The restraint holds
  (=Excluded= IN), and Köln's presumption that the usufructuary has no
  influence (=Powerless=) defeats Oldenburg's inference from the § 1071
  consent right to influence (=CoDetermines=).
- Labellings [2]-[5] are the position of Stuttgart and Oldenburg. The
  issue is reached through =CoDetermines= and the need for registration.
  They differ in two further choices, which are independent of each other:
  - =ConsentConstitutive=. Stuttgart and the appellant before München
    derive constitutive effect from § 1071. München (only damages) and
    Köln (created without the partnership) refute it. Neither side
    outranks the other, so the stable labellings take each side once.
    Where constitutive effect holds, it defeats =Powerless= ([4], [5]).
  - =LikeExecutorship=. Stuttgart's analogy with the executorship needs
    =CoDetermines=. Where that holds, the analogy is complete and meets
    München's distinction (=Unlike=) head on. Again neither outranks the
    other ([3], [5] against [2], [4]).
- So the pro side counts four labellings, 2 × 2 for the two open
  questions. The con side counts one, because it needs both questions
  decided against the usufructuary.

Verdicts:
- Skeptically (stable) the issue is open: =?UNDEC:Registrable usufruct=.
- Credulously it is a value, with four accepting witnesses ([2]-[5]).
  Their normal forms alternate between the need route (=app_registrable=,
  through § 1071) and the analogy route (=muc_hyp_analogy=).
- Grounded leaves =Registrable= undecided.

** The IRIS § 2.2.2 hypothesis

AI NOTE: the hypothesis that each court's position is an extension holds
here.
- Both outcomes are stable labellings of the one framework: the con
  outcome as [1], the pro outcome as [2]-[5].
- The disagreement is localised. Every labelling agrees on liability, on
  the vote in day-to-day affairs, on clarity and on late filings. The
  labellings part only at the consent right's effect (=CoDetermines=,
  =ConsentConstitutive=, =Powerless=) and at the executorship analogy.
  Fragment (f) in =rq3-fragments.org= shows the first point in isolation.
- The pro outcome is not a single position. Stuttgart and Oldenburg reach
  it by different routes. Their four labellings are combinations of
  those routes, not four court positions.
