#+title: IRIS follow-up: AIDA on registering a usufruct on a limited partner's interest
#+language: en

Research material for a short English follow-up paper to the IRIS 2026
overview paper (=IRIS2026 V3 24.12.2025 clean version.docx=, DIREGA).
It gives tentative answers to research questions 3, 4, 6 and 7 with
AIDA. Task: =tasks.org=, task =aida-iris-usufruct=. The phases and their
review gates are listed there.

Authorship: the paper's prose is the authors'. These files hold:
- the decision texts
- AI draft translations marked =AI DRAFT TRANSLATION — not reviewed by a lawyer=
- formalisations
- comments marked =AI NOTE=

AI NOTE on the burden of proof (for Phase 4, needs legal review):
- Register proceedings are FamFG proceedings, where the court inquires
  ex officio (§ 26 FamFG). So there is no burden of proof on a party in
  the adversarial sense.
- What remains is the objective burden: who bears the risk that a fact
  stays unproven. To that are added the limits of the inquiry: the
  presumption that reported facts are correct, and the documents the law
  requires the applicant to submit.
- The IRIS draft's § 3.1 describes exactly these limits. Phase 4 should
  name them in these terms rather than as a civil-procedure burden.

* Extracts for the paper (Phase 6)

AI NOTE: candidate extracts, each a snippet, a figure and a verdict line,
with the claim it could support. =paper-examples.org= (on GitHub: [[file:paper-examples.md][paper-examples.md]]) assembles them
with their renderings (vanilla and English check list) and decorated
figures. The claims are for the authors to adopt
or reject, and every legal reading still needs review.

** E1 (RQ 3): both outcomes as stable labellings
- Snippet: =rq3-fragments.org=, fragment (g): the 10-line closed debate.
- Figure: =figures/frag_g_graph.png=.
- Verdict: three stable labellings, one con (München, Köln) and two pro
  (Stuttgart, Oldenburg); skeptically open; grounded undecided. The same
  pattern holds across all four decisions in one document: 5 stable
  labellings, 1 con and 4 pro (=rq3-combined.org=, Results).
- Claim: the IRIS § 2.2.2 hypothesis, that each court's position is an
  extension of one framework, holds for the four decisions. The courts
  part at two points only: the effect of the § 1071 consent right, and
  the executorship analogy.

** E2 (RQ 3): refutation as burden allocation
- Snippet: fragment (a), München against the liability ground.
- Figure: =figures/frag_a_graph.png=.
- Verdict: a value, resting on =!IN:Excluded usufruct=.
- Claim: a court that only rebuts the grounds offered still decides the
  case, because the restraint is presumed and the need is owed. AIDA
  shows this as which sites are delegations and which obligations.

** E3 (RQ 4): the gap as a delegation
- Snippet: =rq4-6-7-metanorm.org=, block =duty=, against =duty_owed=.
  The two differ in one line: =by default.= at the meta-norm.
- Verdict: delegated, a value with =!IN:MetaNorm= in the normal form;
  owed, open with =MetaNorm= OUT. A positivist objection and the IRIS
  draft's answer (the written rules presuppose the meta-norm) move the
  verdict as expected (4c, 4d).
- Claim: an unwritten norm the written law presupposes can be
  represented as a delegation. The verdict then names the gap it rests
  on, and the gap stays open to argument.

** E4 (RQ 6): the burden of proof per requirement
- Snippet: block =requirements= and the table of who must act.
- Figure: =figures/duty_graph.png= (scenario 6a).
- Verdict: complete application: a value; missing document: open with
  the missing item named (the interim order); justified doubt: open;
  doubt answered by proof: a value. No opposing presumptions in any
  scenario.
- Claim: the burden of proof, in the general sense of what the applicant
  must show and what the court must examine, becomes the polarity of
  single sites: a presumed or an owed refutation. Its shift after a
  justified doubt needs no further machinery.

** E5 (RQ 7): the circle between claim and scope of examination
- Snippet: block =cycle=.
- Figure: =figures/claim_graph.png= (scenario 7a).
- Verdict: alone, grounded undecided and two stable labellings (claim,
  limited scope and duty all IN, or all OUT); with an independent anchor,
  a value (a presumption from case law, or strict from a statute as in
  § 38 AktG); with a doubt, the claim is OUT.
- Claim: the circular justification the BGH line uses is represented
  without refusal, as two founded readings. An independent anchor
  decides it, and the graph shows whether that anchor is written law or
  a presumption.

** E6 (evaluation): the same material in six other systems
- Snippet: the results matrix of =evaluation/comparison.org=.
- Claim: all systems reach the RQ 4 and RQ 6 verdicts, but they state the
  burden in different places. On the core of the RQ 3 dispute three of
  them decide by tie-breaking. On the RQ 7 circle they range from two
  readings to non-termination.

** E7 (RQ 3): a decision as a course of moves
- Snippet: =rq3-debate-dynamics.org=, the traced Oldenburg debate.
- Verdict: open, open, value, value, value, value, open, value, value,
  value, open, value: each objection of the register court opens the
  issue, and each answer closes it.
- Claim: a suitable model keeps the decision's order of objections and
  answers. AIDA shows it as the state of the debate after every move.

** E8 (RQ 4, RQ 8): the gap as a process
- Snippet: =rq4-gap-dynamics.org=, the case law from the register court
  (2012) to Köln (2019) as one debate.
- Verdict: closed by the restraint; filled for the usufruct by Stuttgart
  and Oldenburg (one labelling, registrable); contested from München on
  (5 labellings, 1 con).
- Claim: gaps are closed tentatively and revised. With an append-only
  record whose statuses change, open enumeration is one more supporting
  argument per example, open structure is one more denial per added
  necessary condition, and what stays open is what the latest decisions
  dispute.

** E9 (RQ 6, "verarbeiten"): the court's order as the evaluation's output
- Snippet: =rq4-6-7-metanorm.org=, layer 2b (block =orders=).
- Verdict: the witness is the entry order (complete application, or
  doubt cleared), the submission order (application missing), or the
  proof order (register contradicts the report). It comes with a
  certificate whose delegations mark what the court presumed.
- Claim: once the debate is complete, the burden of proof is processed.
  The evaluation returns the order to issue, and its unmet obligations
  are the content of an interim order.
- Further (layer 2c): the witness rises to the top even where the
  argument does not name it.
  - Constructively: =decision_c= opens the finding "some required
    document is missing", and cut elimination puts =interim d_appl= at
    the head of the normal form.
  - Classically: =decision_k= proves that some order is lawful through an
    argued Peirce step without deciding whether the entry is owed. The
    labelling decides, and the normal form shows =o_entry= or =o_submit=
    accordingly.
