AI DRAFT (extension of Phase 3, task =aida-iris-usufruct=, after the
author's remarks of 2026-11-14).

- *The question.* A gap is not only a state of the law but a task.
  Practitioners close it tentatively, case by case, by interpretation,
  analogy and distinguishing, and later decisions revise the closure.
  RQ 4 therefore asks how this dynamics can be represented, not only a
  snapshot.
- *Two patterns* (the author's terms):
  - *Open enumeration.* The gap is filled by examples of the concept
    that accumulate: one more fact held registrable, one more held not.
    The concept becomes an open disjunction of its examples, extended by
    analogy.
  - *Open structure.* The concept is constrained by necessary conditions
    and exceptions that later decisions add to examples already
    established.
- *The connection to RQ 8* (open texture, analogy): the second limb of
  the meta-norm, "a substantial need of legal dealings", is an
  open-textured term. The courts even word its threshold differently.
  Its meaning is what the two patterns build up.
- *The material.* The four decisions of RQ 3 and the precedents they cite,
  read as one process in time: a closed debate =lawdev= whose moves come
  in the order the decisions were handed down, with the state queried
  after each stage. The document only grows. What changes is the status
  of its statements.

AI NOTE (limits):
- The thresholds are one statement here. Separating them, as three
  statements with rules between them (=Compelling -> Substantial ->
  Legitimate=), would make the courts' disagreement about the test itself
  arguable. That is not done here.
- A precedent's holding is strict, and its reasons are presumed or owed
  like any other argument. A court that overrules a precedent rather than
  distinguishing it would need the holding to be defeasible.

* The case law as one debate

The debate opens with the register court's restraint (Stuttgart's
register court, 2012): not registrable, as no statute provides for it.
The precedents (BGH 2012 executorship, BGH 1998 deputy note, BGH 2015
GmbH list) are declarations in =vocabulary.org=, so they are in scope
from the start.

Moves without a verb are not checked as replies; they join the scope in
the order given. Within a stack, a later move stands above an earlier
one.

AI NOTE (observations for the authors):
- *Tentative closure, then revision, in one record.* The gap is first
  closed against the usufruct by a presumption (stage 0). It is reopened
  and closed for it by analogy and by the need argument (stages 1-2), and
  reopened by the refutations (stages 3-4). The record only grows, and
  each step is a move. What changes is the status of statements.
- *Open enumeration.* The executorship enters as a positive example and
  is extended to the usufruct by analogy (stage 1). The deputy note and
  the GmbH list enter as negative examples, which refute the
  generalisation "an interest in information suffices" (=InfoSuffices=
  OUT from stage 3). Each example is one more argument, and no earlier
  argument is edited.
- *Open structure.* München adds necessary conditions to the analogy (the
  executor's three features) and to influence (no vote, no constitutive
  consent). The added conditions turn the analogy OUT in three of five
  labellings, and the vote OUT in all. Köln's further reason (created
  without the partnership) changes no reading, since the condition it
  supports was already contested.
- *What stays open is exactly what the later decisions dispute.*
  Constitutive effect and the analogy remain undecided between the
  courts. The two open questions of RQ 3 (=rq3-combined.org=) are the
  residue of this process.
- *Redundancy.* Oldenburg's second route changes nothing at stage 2. It
  is what keeps four of five readings pro after München refutes the
  vote. A model that records only verdicts would not show why a later
  refutation does not end the matter.
