AI DRAFT (extension of Phase 3, task =aida-iris-usufruct=, after the
author's remarks of 2026-11-14 and 2026-11-15).

- *The question.* A gap is not only a state of the law but a task.
  Practitioners close it tentatively, case by case, by interpretation,
  analogy and distinguishing, and later decisions revise the closure.
  RQ 4 therefore asks how this dynamics can be represented, not only a
  snapshot.
- *Two patterns* (the author's terms):
  - *Open enumeration.* The gap is filled by examples of the concept
    that accumulate: one more fact held registrable, one more held not.
    The concept becomes an open disjunction of its examples, extended by
    analogy.
  - *Open structure.* The concept is constrained by necessary conditions
    and exceptions that later decisions add to examples already
    established.
- *The example* is the one RQ 4 starts from (=rq4-6-7-metanorm.org=):
  the missing meta-norm that obliges the register court to enter a fact.
  It is followed as one process in two parts.
  - *Part 1: the meta-norm takes shape.* The steps by which, according
    to the IRIS draft (§§ 3.1-3.2 and the decisions and commentaries it
    cites), the gap was closed: the meta-norm presupposed, the
    requirements of the examination added one by one, the power to
    examine and its limits, the claim to registration and the circle
    between claim and scope (RQ 7), and finally the draft's candidate
    wording. Each step is asked for two entries side by side:
    - C's entry as limited partner, which a statute provides for;
    - the usufruct on the limited partner's interest, which no statute
      provides for.
  - *Part 2: the open limb, argued.* The candidate's second limb,
    abstract registrability by "a substantial need of legal dealings",
    is an open-textured term. For the usufruct, Part 1 ends open at
    exactly that limb. The four decisions of RQ 3, read in the order
    they were handed down, are the process in which it is argued.
- *The connection to RQ 8* (open texture, analogy): the courts even word
  the threshold of the second limb differently. Its meaning is what the
  two patterns of Part 2 build up.
- *How it is represented.* Each part is one closed debate per question,
  whose moves come in the order of the process, with the state queried
  after each stage. The record only grows. What changes is the status
  of its statements.

** C's entry, stage by stage

*** Stage 0: no written meta-norm (§ 3.1; note 19)

No written norm orders the register court to enter a fact, and
proposals to codify the requirements were held unnecessary (the
legislative materials of the 1980 GmbH reform, note 19). The duty
argument hands the meta-norm on as a delegation: =MetaNorm e1=, the
meta-norm applied to C's entry, presumed. Nothing yet conditions it.

*** Stage 1: only a written norm binds

The objection the draft's § 3.2 answers: without a written norm there
is no duty. No source in the draft makes it; it is the positivist
reading of note 19's silence. It attacks the norm's existence
(=InForce=), so the meta-norm's first condition is moved with it: it
yields a duty only if it is in force (=draft_mn_in_force=).

#+begin_src fspy :tangle build/rq4-metanorm-dynamics.fspy
draft_mn_in_force.
nosrc_positivist.
evaluate meta_c stable.
#+end_src

every appeal; § 9c (2) GmbHG (1980) and § 38 (2) AktG only limit an

*** Stage 3: the formal requirements (notes 20, 21)

From the rule-of-law principle the court examines the formal
requirements, among them the abstract registrability of the fact. The
restraint makes registrability something the applicants have to
establish (its refutation is presumed in =lit_no_reg=); for C, §§ 162 (1),
106 (2), 107 HGB establish it (=hgb162_reg=). The documents the law requires
are a formal requirement too (§ 12 (1) HGB: =hgb12_no_doc=), and the
application is submitted (=case_doc_appl=). The requirements enter the
meta-norm as its second condition: it yields a duty only if the
examination is passed (=draft_mn_passed=).

lit_no_reg.
hgb162_reg.
hgb12_no_doc.

evaluate meta_c stable.
#+end_src

*** Stage 4: the substantive requirements and the power to examine (notes 22, 25, 26)

The court also examines whether the reported facts are correct (=bgh2011_no_cor=,
its refutation owed: the court must find a reason). The power to do so
follows from its duty to avoid incorrect entries (BGH NJW-RR 2011, 1184
Rn. 10). On the level of evidence it is restricted: the court has to
assume that the reported facts are correct (=bgh2011_face_value=; BGH 2011 and
BayObLGZ 1982, 198). The court of this case uses its power: it demands
the transfer agreement, which the law does not require (=case_court_extra=).
That the demand is within its examination is presumed. Its reason is the
court's own argument against correctness (=case_court_flaw=): an error
in the agreement would show the report incorrect. That the agreement
shows an error is owed, and nobody can supply it without the agreement,
so the presumption of correctness stands; the demand defeats the
examination on its own, because the agreement is not submitted.

#+begin_src fspy :tangle build/rq4-metanorm-dynamics.fspy
bgh2011_no_cor.
bgh2011_face_value.
case_court_flaw.
case_court_extra.
evaluate meta_c stable.
#+end_src

*** Stage 5: the claim to registration and the circle (notes 28-31)

BGH NJW 1991, 1754 (1758) recognises an unwritten claim to registration
and limits the scope of examination with it (=bgh1991_claim_limits=, =bgh1991_improper_demand=:
a limited scope makes the demand improper). The limit has two
exceptions, each a denial of the presumption that it applies to the
demand: the law requires the document (=case_court_required=), or there
are concrete indications that the report is incorrect
(=bgh1991_indication=, supplied by =case_indication= from a conflict
with the register). In C's case both fail: no statute requires the
transfer agreement, and nothing in the register contradicts the report.
Then the inference runs both ways:
- from the claim to the limited scope (OLG Stuttgart 2020, on § 9c
  GmbHG);
- from the limited scope to the claim (Ammon 1995 for the GmbH; Krafka
  2004 for every register procedure).

Duty, power of examination and claim are taken always to correspond
(Wicke 2022). In the encoding the claim follows from the duty (=lit_corr_claim=,
correlativity) and the duty from the claim (=lit_corr_duty=).

lit_corr_duty.

*** Stage 7: the candidate wording (§ 3.2; notes 34, 35)

The draft's candidate assembles the steps: the court has to enter a
fact if the formal and substantive requirements are met (=bridge=,
=Passed=); abstract registrability is among the formal requirements
(=lit_no_reg=); abstractly registrable are facts a statute provides for
(=reg_stat=) or for which legal dealings have a substantial need
(=reg_need=). The second limb is taken from the four OLG decisions
(note 35), which trace it to BGH NZG 2012, 385 Rn. 16. For C nothing is
added: its entry is provided by statute.

AI NOTE (observations for the authors):
- *Tentative closure, then revision, in one record.* At stage 0 the gap
  is closed by a bare presumption, and the closure is over-inclusive:
  the usufruct would have to be entered too. Stages 1 and 2 reopen and
  close it again. From stage 3 on, each requirement added to the
  examination narrows what the presumed meta-norm yields, and the
  usufruct drops out at the first of them.
- *Widening, then narrowing.* The power to examine lets the court's
  demand defeat the examination (stage 4). The claim offers a limit, but
  one that only supports itself: the circle leaves two readings (stage
  5), the formal counterpart of the draft's observation that duty, power
  and claim were assumed always to correspond. The normative scope,
  which does not depend on the claim, decides it (stage 6). These are
  scenarios 7a and 7b of =rq4-6-7-metanorm.org=, now as two moments of
  one process, and in the order of the sources (BGH 1991, Ammon 1995,
  OLG Karlsruhe 2001).
- *The red thread.* The candidate's second limb adds nothing for C and
  changes, for the usufruct, only where the failure lies: from "no
  statute provides for it" to "nobody has shown a substantial need".
  That owed need is the question of Part 2 and of RQ 3.
- *Open enumeration by kind.* The registrable facts are enumerated by
  limb: those a statute provides for, then those with a substantial
  need. Each limb is one more argument for =Registrable=.

AI NOTE (limits, for legal review):
- The sources of the circle (notes 28-31) concern the GmbH, except
  Krafka 2004, who extends the inference to every register procedure.
  The encoding states the rules for every entry, as the draft's summary
  does. A finer model would record the claim for GmbH procedures and its
  generalisation as a step of open enumeration of its own, and would
  show that for a limited partnership the circle depends on that
  generalisation.
- The court's demand for the transfer agreement is an illustration
  (layer 3 of =rq4-6-7-metanorm.org=), not a fact of a reported case.
- Stage 1's objection has no source in the draft.

* Part 2: the open limb, argued for the usufruct (2012-2019)

The question Part 1 leaves open, whether legal dealings have a
substantial need to know of the usufruct, is the question of the four
decisions of RQ 3 and the precedents they cite. They are read here as
one process in time: a closed debate =lawdev= whose moves come in the
order the decisions were handed down. Part 2 uses the propositional
vocabulary of RQ 3 (=vocabulary.org=), in which =Registrable [usufruct]=
is the statement Part 1 writes =Registrable uf=. The two parts are
separate scripts.

register court, 2012): not registrable, as no statute provides for it.
The precedents (BGH 2012 executorship, BGH 1998 deputy note, BGH 2015
GmbH list) are declarations in =vocabulary.org=, so they are in scope
from the start.

Moves without a verb are not checked as replies; they join the scope in
the order given. Within a stack, a later move stands above an earlier
one.
