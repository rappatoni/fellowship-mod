author's remarks of 2026-11-14).

RQ 3 asks for a /geeignete Modellierung/: a suitable model of the courts'
argumentation. Phase 2 checked that the abstract argumentative situation
comes out right (the courts' positions as labellings). A suitable model
has to do more:
- *The debate itself.* The order of the arguments, who replies to whom,
  how the state of the debate evolves move by move, and how the
  decision's text is structured.
- *Its readers.* Whether people can follow, explore and extend it: a
  natural-language rendering, exploring subarguments, graphs, an account
  of the evaluation, and adding new arguments.

This file checks these points for AIDA. =evaluation/comparison.org=
compares them across the tools.

* The course of a debate, move by move

AIDA compiles a debate as it stands while it is being recorded, so a
query between two moves shows the state after the first. The two traced
debates below repeat the courts' debates of =rq3-oldenburg.org= and
=rq3-muenchen.org= move for move, with the verbs (attack, support,
undermine) the courts' replies have, and evaluate after every move.

How to read the classes:
- In a =pro= debate (Oldenburg) a value means the issue is established,
  and open means it is not (yet).
- In a =con= debate (München) a value means the refutation stands. An
  exception means the refutation aborts because the other side
  prevails, and open means undecided.

AI NOTE (observations for the authors):
- *The state of the debate is visible move by move.* Oldenburg's text
  is a sequence of objections and answers, and the trace alternates open
  and value accordingly. The decision's structure becomes a trajectory.
- *München's decision turns late.* Its specific refutations (moves
  13-21) do not yet restore its outcome even credulously. What does is
  the defeat of the generalisation "an interest in information
  suffices", by the BGH's counter-instances (move 22). Read this way, the
  counter-instances carry München's decision, more than the refutations
  of the participation rights do. Whether that matches the court's own
  weighting is a question for the legal reviewer.
- *Order and replies are part of the model.*
  - A debate keeps who replied to whom (the verbs are checked against
    their targets) and in which order.
  - The order also matters for evaluation: within a stack, the later
    move is judged first.
  - The literate files keep each move next to the paragraph it
    formalises (the paragraph =CUSTOM_ID=s of =sources/=).

** Reading the debate

What a reader gets, tried on =read_c=:

| Means | What it gives | Assessment |
|-------+---------------+------------|
| =render … argumentation= | nested text with the decorated statements, "We will argue for …", "by default! …" | readable in outline; mixes in the German check-list phrases ("Für … ist hinreichend, dass", "weil", "und", "gegen … spricht"); shows internal names (=alt1=, =r_3=, =H1=, =_F_=); deep nesting |
| =render … pruefschema= | a German check-list ("Es genügt zu prüfen, dass …", "sofern nichts entgegensteht: …", "Prüfpunkt") | the frame is the one German lawyers use for the order of examination; the statements stay English (decorations are English); in the unevaluated term an unsupported site shows as "Prüfung fehlgeschlagen" |
| =render … dialectical= | the scaffold term (=SUP=, =ATT=, =PUS=) with proof terms | for experts on the calculus only |
| =render … evaluated= | the normal form as text: what the verdict rests on | the most useful for a reader: one line of reasons per kept step |
| =explain= | the pipeline's account, stage by stage | a developer trace; its =sigma= lines are readable reasons ("attacker … is OUT -> keep the original") |
| =graph … show= | a picture with labels, onus markings (=[t:p]=, =[c:o]=) and a legend | legible for small debates (fragments); large ones are unwieldy (=figures/claim_graph.png=) |
| =label= | the labelling, statement by statement | complete but long |

AI NOTE (assessment):
- *For a legal reader, AIDA is not ready.* It has every ingredient: a
  rendering per argument and per normal form, check-list structure,
  decorations, a graph with a legend, and incremental queries. But the
  text output mixes languages and leaks internal names.
  - A rendering for this experiment's audience would need an English
    counterpart of the check-list frame, and the internal binders left
    out (task =aida-render-english-argumentation= on the branch).
  - The acceptance-tree renderer is deprecated.
- *Exploration.* Subarguments are named and can be targeted (=render=,
  =graph=, =evaluate= take any argument or debate). The interactive CLI
  and the Emacs integration let a reader add an argument and re-query.
  The document graph is global, so a new counterargument contests every
  use of its statement at once. This was not tested with users.
- *Incremental addition.* Arguments are added, never edited. A debate
  can be queried while it is recorded. The combined document arises by
  including the four court files, and the case-law debate of
  =rq4-gap-dynamics.org= by adding moves in time. On main, a registered
  debate can also be extended (=debate X extends Y=) without changing
  its parent. That is not on this branch.
- *The legal text's control flow (cf. Catala).* Catala mirrors a
  statute's structure (scopes, default rules with exceptions in the
  order of the text) and computes with it. AIDA mirrors the reasoning of
  a decision: its order of moves and its replies. A statute's own
  structure is represented here only through the meta-norm's helper and
  its denials (one per requirement), which is a check-list. The
  =pruefschema= rendering prints it in the order of examination. Catala
  was not run (it is outside the plan's comparison set); the DIREGA
  group has Catala material in =DIREGA/formalizations/catala=.
