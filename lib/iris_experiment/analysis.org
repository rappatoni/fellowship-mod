* Decisions recorded with the author

- 2026-11-13: "burden of proof" is used in a general sense here. It
  means the standard of evidence the applicant has to meet and,
  dually, the amount and depth of examination (due diligence) the
  register court performs. This is not the civil-procedure burden.
- 2026-11-13, conjunction encoding:
  - Default: a helper statement that holds by default, with one denial
    per conjunct.
  - Where a real conjunction is needed: the derived =A - ~B= rather
    than currying.
  - Transpositions are added by hand.
  - Preference is the order of arguments within each stack.
  - No opposing presumptions is a consistency condition.
- 2026-11-16, necessary conditions: the subtraction on the context side
  is the connective for a necessary condition. "H requires C" is
  =deny d : (H - C)=, used directly: a counterargument against H builds
  =H - C= from H and C's refutation (presumed or owed) and closes with
  =moxia d=. No implication =H -> C= applied by modus tollens.
  - At first order the denial is a denied existential,
    =deny d : (exists e:Ev, H [e] - C [e])=: the refutation of an
    existential is the universal of refutations, so one declaration
    covers every instance.
  - The owed site is C's refutation itself, the node an objection to C
    lands on; a premise =~C= would be a node of its own, unconnected
    until the negation bridge exists (=tasks.org=, BACKLOG).
  - Where a premise guards the condition (the entry's reification
    =Enter e c f=, a document being required or properly demanded, a
    principle like =LiabilityOnly=), the guard stays a premise and the
    condition is the subtraction under it: =∀…, G -> ~(H - C)=, a
    negated subtraction on the term side, i.e. the subtraction on the
    context side. The proof cuts the subtraction first (=cut (H - C) th.=),
    since AIDA's term grammar cannot read the other order
    (=aida-derived-conjunction=).
  - Applied across the experiment on 2026-11-16: the meta-norm's
    conditions (denied existentials), the requirements of the
    examination (=n_reg=, =n_doc=, =n_cor=, =n_dem=, guarded),
    München's membership conditions (=muc_no_mgmt=, =muc_no_vote=,
    =muc_no_liab=, =muc_no_repr=: a presumed denial in place of a
    presumed rule) and =p_liab_only= (Oldenburg, Stuttgart). Left as
    they are: the counterexamples to a principle (=muc_counter_deputy=,
    =muc_counter_gmbh=), which refute =InfoSuffices= through instances
    of principles used forwards elsewhere, not a necessary condition;
    and the spikes of § 5, a record of 2026-11-13.

AI NOTE (structure):
- From II.2 b) on, every subsection rebuts one ground that the appeal,
  OLG Stuttgart or OLG Oldenburg offered for a need: voting rights,
  § 1071, liability, representation, the executor analogy.
- The only positive step is M4: because no compelling need is shown, the
  fact is not registrable.
- In AIDA's terms this is a burden allocation. The need's refutation is
  presumed, so the applicant must establish the need, and the court
  only rebuts the grounds offered. That is the "refutation-only"
  pattern of the IRIS draft (§ 2.2.1), and it falls out of the helper
  encoding with a presumed refutation (spike, section 5).

** Cross-court view

- *Same meta-norm, different thresholds.* All courts use "registrable if
  provided by statute or if there is a need of legal dealings". The
  threshold differs:
  - München: "unabweisbar" (compelling)
  - Oldenburg and Köln: "erheblich" (substantial)
  - Stuttgart: "berechtigtes Interesse" (legitimate interest)

  For RQ 3 these become distinct statements. Which test applies is
  itself a point of disagreement (open texture, RQ 8 of the IRIS draft).
- *Grounds for a need* are alternatives, so they become separate
  supporters: liability, representation, influence (vote; § 1071),
  analogy (executor; GmbH list and share register, which no court uses),
  the register court's own need for information.
- *Disputed sub-issues* (the attack targets):
  - ExternalLiability: München, Köln: no. Staub/Schäfer: yes.
  - VotingRightDayToDay: Stuttgart: yes. München, Köln: no. Oldenburg: left open.
  - Consent1071 suffices for influence: Oldenburg: yes. München, Köln: no, not constitutive.
  - ExecutorAnalogy: München: distinguished.
  - DelayedFilings: Oldenburg: rebutted.
- *Priority.* München states one explicitly and defeasibly: register
  clarity "regularly" takes precedence over the interest in
  information. This is the only place where precedence is argued
  rather than presupposed.
  - In AIDA's natural preference this matches the default: the attack
    stack goes on top of the support stack, so clarity, as an attacker
    of the need, wins.
  - A court that wanted the need to prevail would need the top/bottom
    switch (task =aida-support-attack-priority-switch=). Oldenburg does
    not argue that. It argues that influence is a need clarity does not
    exclude (O4).

* 3. Choice of logical base

Decision (proposed for R1):
- *RQ 3: propositional.*
  - Each court's argument relates closed propositions about the one
    case before it.
  - The general principles the courts invoke (the meta-norm, the
    reductio in M11) enter as rules over those propositions.
  - Nothing in the four decisions needs to quantify over individuals
    to be represented faithfully.
  - Propositional debates also use AIDA's most mature part: the
    shared route, every semantics, rendering.
- *RQ 4, 6, 7: first order with reified eventualities and deontic
  predicates.*
  - The norms there are general: "for every fact reported", "for
    every entry e, the claim to e and the duty to make e correspond",
    and the meta-norm for "a fact".
  - The burden question is a per-fact presumption (N6). The spike
    shows that a generic argument with a discourse referent expresses
    it directly: =face_value : Correct [?f]= by default reaches the
    instance =Correct uf=.
  - The spike (section 5) runs every needed feature.
  - Cost: these debates must use =pipeline unfolded.= (referents).

Deontic encoding: reification after Hobbs (1985) as used by Robaldo &
Sun (2017) and the DAPRECO knowledge base (Robaldo et al. 2020); the
references are checked in =README.org=.
- An eventuality is a term of sort =Ev=. =Enter [e] [c] [f]= reads "e is
  an entering of fact f by c". In Hobbs's notation that is
  =enter'(e, c, f)=. Its real existence (=Rexist e=) is folded into the
  predicate, because AIDA has no conjunction (as in
  =eval/literature3/fo_rio.fspy=).
- Deontic notions are ordinary predicates over agents and eventuality
  terms: =Duty [c] [a] [e]= ("c owes a that e is realised") and
  =Claim [a] [c] [e]= ("a can claim from c that e is realised"). So the
  classical calculus carries them with no modal operator.
- Hohfeld's correlativity is two universally quantified rules, because
  AIDA has no biconditional: =Duty b a e -> Claim a b e= and
  =Claim a b e -> Duty b a e=. Correlation therefore creates the
  derivation cycle of RQ 7 as soon as both directions are argued.

AI NOTE: this encoding keeps deontic reasoning *inside* the
argumentation.
- An obligation is a statement that can be presumed, owed, attacked or
  supported like any other.
- There is no detachment rule of a deontic logic.
- Whether that is enough for the paper's claims (for example, that a
  conflict of duties is not a contradiction) should be checked when
  the RQ 7 example is written.

* 4. Conjunction encodings compared

The spike runs all three on "fly and feathers ⇒ bird", with Fly proven
and nothing said about Feathers, then with Feathers refuted. Outcomes
are in section 5.

| Criterion | Helper + one denial per conjunct | Derived =A - ~B= | Curried =A -> B -> C= |
|---+---+---+---|
| Open to a further conjunct | yes: one more denial and counterargument; the existing arguments are unchanged | no: the rule names its conjuncts | no: a new rule; the old strict rule stays valid |
| Burden per conjunct | yes, chosen *per denial*: the counterargument's site on the conjunct's refutation is a delegation (the proponent must prove the conjunct) or an obligation (an opponent must refute it) | yes, per conjunct site: =by default= or =next= | yes, per premise site |
| What an attack on a conjunct defeats | the helper (one node), and with it every consequent derived from it | the conjunction node =A-~B= (a lemma of its own, =bird.c1=) | the argument directly |
| Graph | helper node, one attacker per conjunct; necessary conditions visible as attackers | a conjunction node with its two conjunct sources | no conjunction node; conjuncts are premises of the rule's edge |
| Reading | the helper is "jointly sufficient", a due-diligence check: passed unless a necessary condition fails | "A and B" as one statement others can cite | a chain of conditions |
| Cost | 1 helper + 1 rule + n denials + n counterarguments | 1 rule; tactic order matters (see the AIDA issue below) | 1 rule |
| First order | works: denial as a universal implication =forall ..., H -> C= (equivalent to =deny (H - C)= classically), one generic or instance counterargument per conjunct | not tried at first order | works |
| Later derived ∧ (task =aida-derived-conjunction=) | no migration needed; a different idiom | direct: the derived ∧ is meant to replace it | rewrite needed |

AIDA issue found:
- With the tactic order =cut (rule) …; axiom r; elim; elim; …=,
  Fellowship prints the subtraction introduction as the left operand of
  a stack, =1.2.1.1!*!1.2.1.2*rr=, without parentheses. AIDA's term
  grammar (=core/ac/syntax.py=, =cons: tight_term _STAR context=)
  cannot read it ("Unexpected token '*'"), so the argument is not
  registered.
- Cutting the subtraction first (=cut (Fly - ~Feathers) c. elim. …=)
  avoids it.
- Recorded under task =aida-derived-conjunction=.

Recommendation (for R1):
1. *Helper* wherever the conditions form a list the law or the courts
   may extend, and wherever the burden differs between conditions:
   - the meta-norm's requirements (N2, N3, N10)
   - the requirements whose proof the applicant owes, and those whose
     refutation the court owes (RQ 6)

   This is the default.
2. *Derived =A - ~B=* where a conjunction is itself a statement that
   other arguments cite as a whole, and closedness is intended.
   Candidate: "formal and substantive requirements are met" if a court
   refers to that pair as one thing. None is needed so far.
3. *Currying* only for a closed list fixed by its source, with no one
   arguing about its parts or extending it. In the four decisions,
   nearly every rule has a single premise ("no liability ⇒ no need to
   disclose liability"), so currying hardly arises. Where a premise
   pair occurs in a court's chain, use 1 or 2 rather than currying,
   so the chain stays open to the other courts' grounds.

** Conjunction encodings (propositional)

Helper encoding. Fly's refutation is presumed, so the proponent must
prove Fly. Feathers' refutation is owed, so an opponent must refute
Feathers.

#+begin_src fspy :tangle build/spike-helper.fspy
lk.
declare Fly, Feathers, Bird, H : bool.
declare r1 : (H -> Bird).
deny d_fly : (H - Fly).
deny d_feathers : (H - Feathers).
declare ax_fly : (Fly).

argument bird : (Bird).
cut (H -> Bird) r.
axiom r1.
elim.
by default.
next.
axiom r.
dixi.

counterargument no_fly : (H).
cut (H - Fly) th.
elim.
by default.
next.
axiom th.
moxia d_fly.
dixi.

counterargument no_feathers : (H).
cut (H - Feathers) th.
elim.
next.
axiom th.
moxia d_feathers.
dixi.

label bird.
evaluate bird.

argument fly : (Fly).
axiom ax_fly.
dixi.

label bird.
evaluate bird.
#+end_src

Outcomes:
- Before =fly=: =Fly[c]= IN (presumed refutation, unanswered),
  =H[c]= IN, =Bird= OUT; =evaluate bird= is OPEN, =?OUT:Bird=.
- After =fly=: =Fly[c]= OUT, =Feathers[c]= OUT (owed, unsupplied),
  =H= IN, =Bird= IN; a VALUE, =μbird:Bird.<r1:H->Bird||!IN:H*bird:Bird>=.
- With a strict refutation of Feathers added, =Feathers[c]= IN, =H= and
  =Bird= OUT, OPEN.

Derived =A - ~B=. The subtraction is cut first; the tactic order is
the point.

#+begin_src fspy :tangle build/spike-subtraction.fspy
lk.
declare Fly, Feathers, Bird : bool.

declare ax_fly : (Fly).
declare ax_nofeathers : (~Feathers).

elim.
by default.
next.
by default.
next.
cut ((Fly - ~Feathers) -> Bird) rr.
axiom r.
elim.
axiom.
axiom c.
dixi.

argument fly : (Fly).
axiom ax_fly.
dixi.

label bird.
evaluate bird.

dixi.

Outcomes:
- The conjunction becomes a node of its own,
  =bird.c1: Fly-~Feathers[t] <- Fly[t:pres], ~Feathers[c:pres]=.
- With =fly=: everything IN; a VALUE.
- With =featherless=: =~Feathers[c]= OUT, so =Fly-~Feathers= and =Bird=
  are OUT; OPEN.

Observations for the later phases:
- *RQ 6:* the per-condition burden is the choice between a delegation
  and an obligation inside the denial's counterargument:
  - =no_reg= presumes the refutation of registrability, so the
    applicant must establish it.
  - =no_cor= owes the refutation of correctness, so the court must
    find a doubt.
  - =face_value= is generic and reaches =Correct uf= as an instance,
    which is N6.
  - A doubt has to rest on something. A *presumed* refutation of
    =Correct uf= would oppose =face_value='s presumption. That is
    exactly the inconsistency the author ruled out, and AIDA does not
    refuse it yet (task =aida-onus-delegation-polarity=).
  - My first draft of =need= made this mistake: it presumed
    =Registrable uf= while =no_reg= presumed its refutation, and both
    went UNDEC. The final version derives registrability from a
    presumed substantial need instead.
- *RQ 7:* once the examination fails, the two correlativity arguments
  keep each other alive:
  - grounded and skeptical: UNDEC, open
  - credulous: an unfounded acceptance, the bare delegation of the
    claim
  - stable (the founded reading): OUT

  So the circular argument the BGH line runs (N8) is visible as an
  unfounded cycle, and an independent anchor (N9) is what would found
  it. This is to be built in Phase 5.
- *Rendering:* =render … pruefschema= produces the German check-list
  frame ("Es genügt zu prüfen, dass …", "sofern nichts entgegensteht:
  …") but prints predicates raw (=Duty court kg e1=). For the English
  paper, =argumentation= with =decorate= templates is the better
  choice.
- *Not yet tried:* the order of arguments deciding among attackers.
  It is checked in Phase 2 where a court's weighing needs it.
- A transposed (contraposed) argument added by hand registers and
  attacks as expected (=no_pass_from_no_duty=: =Passed e1[c] <- …,
  Duty court kg e1[c:obli]=).
