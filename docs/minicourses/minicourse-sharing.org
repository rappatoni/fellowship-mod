* About this sequel

minicourse-evaluation.org describes how a debate is unfolded from the
document graph into one term (lesson 8a) and follows that term through
evaluation (lessons 9-13). Both describe the *unfolded* route.
Since 2026-10-02 it is no longer the route the CLI takes by default.

*Note (2026-10-06).* The default has moved back to the unfolded route
for now: only that route builds the stacked shape and the argument
entrypoints of lesson 8a (tasks.org, aida-unfold-entrypoints). The
shared route described here still builds the *legacy* shape - the site
innermost, each supporter and attacker wrapping what came before - and
compares itself with the legacy unfolding (=unfold_legacy=), until it is
ported (aida-shared-route-stack-shape). To follow these lessons, select
it with =pipeline shared.= (or =FSP_PIPELINE=shared=); the transcripts
below were taken on it.

The reason is size. Unfolding copies a statement's debate into every
place the statement is needed, so the term is as large as the number of
paths through the document graph. With two alternative supporters per
level, the number of paths doubles per level:

| levels | definitions | shared form (nodes) | unfolded term (nodes) |
|--------+-------------+---------------------+-----------------------|
|      1 |           2 |                  30 |                    29 |
|      2 |           3 |                  59 |                    85 |
|      3 |           4 |                  88 |                   197 |
|      4 |           5 |                 117 |                   421 |
|      8 |           9 |                 233 |                  7141 |

(measured on the generated documents of tests/test_share.py; the term
follows size(n) = 2 size(n-1) + 27, so sixteen levels give about 1.8
million nodes). Before the sharing work, =label= on ten levels - twenty
arguments - took 105 seconds. Now it takes about one.

The pipeline on the default route:

:   document graph, issue
:     |  share               core/dc/share.py         one definition per statement, citations
:     |  typecheck_shared    core/dc/typecheck.py     replay each definition once
:     |  IssueResolver       core/dc/instances.py     compile and resolve each INSTANCE once
:     |  labellings          core/comp/adf_label.py   as before, on the same issue graph
:     |  evaluate_shared     core/comp/evaluate.py    sigma opens a sub-debate only where it is kept
:     v  normalize_strong, classify_nf                as before

Four decisions shape it (author, 2026-10-01):

1. *Names are transparent.* A named sub-debate means its expansion;
   nothing new enters the calculus.
2. *One debate per statement*, that is, per (proposition, side).
3. *A name is shown from the second occurrence on*; a sub-debate used
   once stays in place.
4. *Capture does not force copying.* A citation records what its site
   does to the cited debate.

And one about what may be memoised: not a definition, but an *instance*
(lesson 17). The lemma "normalise the definition once, then plug it in"
is false, since strictness is read off the concrete term and the same
sub-debate is decided differently under different captures.

The unfolded route remains, in its legacy shape, as the reference the
shared one is tested against: =pipeline unfolded.= (or
=FSP_PIPELINE=unfolded=) selects the unfolded route, =pipeline shared.=
this one. Against the legacy unfolding the shared route gives the same
graph, labels and normal form, up to the names of binders and the
numbers of sites.

Prerequisites: lesson 2 (hyperedges), lesson 7 (sigma), and lessons 8a,
10, 11 and 13 of the first sequel (unfolding, the strict phase, sigma's
walk, capture).

* Lesson 14 - Definitions and citations

** Definition

=share(graph, issue)= (=core/dc/share.py=) builds one *definition* for
every statement the issue reaches: the statement's own site, wrapped in
a support scaffold per edge deriving it and an attack scaffold per edge
deriving its contrary - exactly what the unfolder's =_wrap= builds, with
the unfolder's own scaffold code. The difference is what happens at a
site inside an edge's body. The unfolder expands the site's statement in
place, in scope. =share= leaves a *citation* there: a leaf that names the
statement and will be expanded later, if at all.

A citation is a term- or context-side name leaf (=§= internally) that
records three things about its site: the statement cited, whether the
site was a presumption, and what the site's scope does to the cited
debate (lesson 15). A statement whose definition is a bare site - nobody
derives or contests it - is *trivial* and is written in place as that
site.

*Expansion.* =SharedDebate.expand()= writes every definition back in,
interpreting each citation as the unfolder would: a captured statement
becomes the capturing variable, a statement already being expanded on
the path becomes a bare site, any other becomes the definition's body
with its binders renamed on reuse. The result is the unfolded term up to
alpha-equivalence and site numbering, and that equation is the gate the
whole route is tested against: =expand= must agree with =unfold= on
every fixture, and does.

*Names.* A definition is shown by name when it is cited from two or more
sites along the issue's expansion, or when the author cited it with
=cite NAME=. The name is the registered argument or debate whose issue
is the statement, if there is exactly one; otherwise =anon_1=, =anon_2=,
..., numbered on first use and kept with the document, so the numbers do
not shift between commands. =anon_= is a reserved prefix. A sub-debate
needed once is printed in place, and a trivial one is never named.

*What =share ARG.= prints:* the issue's term, in which every named
sub-debate is a citation, and then one line

: NAME[open sites] := body

per named definition. The header lists the sites the sub-debate rests on
when nothing outside captures them.

* Lesson 15 - Captures and cuts

** Definition

The unfolder does three different things at a site, depending on its
scope (lesson 13): a statement bound by an enclosing binder becomes that
variable (*captured*); a statement already being expanded on the current
path stays a bare site (*cut*); any other is expanded. A definition is
built with an empty scope, so inside it only its own binders capture. A
citation therefore has to carry what the unfolder would have known at
that site: =captures=, a map from statements to the binder names that
capture them, and =cuts=, the statements on the path. Expansion
accumulates them from the outside in, inner ones overriding, and applies
the unfolder's three cases in the unfolder's order: captured first, then
cut, then expanded.

Printed, a citation shows that information in brackets, in the author's
notation:

: d[alpha -> !:A, B:?]

reads "d, with alpha capturing its delegation of A, and its obligation B
left open": an arrow marks a captured statement and names the capturing
binder; a bare marker is a cut. The markers are the term printer's:
=?:A= and =!:A= for an obligation and a presumption on the term side,
=A:?= and =A:!= on the context side. A citation of a sub-debate that
nothing captures or cuts shows no bracket at all, as =anon_1= in lesson
14.

A capture can hit *any* statement the sub-debate reaches, not only a
leaf. In the even loop, "P fails" inside q is P's own continuation, and
the whole debate about "P fails" collapses to that variable. That is why
capture does not force copying: the citation says what collapses, and
expansion collapses it.

*A definition is larger than any of its uses.* Because a definition is
built without a scope, it expands statements that every citation of it
captures. The part it expands is dead at each such site: expansion puts
the variable there, not the body. What is actually compiled and resolved
is never the definition but an *instance* of it, the definition under a
given set of captures and cuts (lesson 17).

* Lesson 16 - The type check, one definition at a time

** Definition

The type check (lesson 9: Fellowship replays the term and the two shapes
are compared) replayed the unfolded term, and so grew with it. Now it
replays each definition once. The unit is the definition's *skeleton*:
its body with every citation left as the open site of the cited
statement, so a cited sub-debate is simply a hole of the right type.

This is sound because of what expansion puts at a citation: the cited
definition's body, which is a term or context of the statement's type; a
variable bound for that statement; or the statement's bare site. All
three have the type of the hole. So if every skeleton is well-typed,
every expansion is.

=typecheck_shared= (=core/dc/typecheck.py=) replays the skeletons of all
reachable definitions, skipping the trivial ones, and remembers what
replayed in the document (=prover.typechecked()=): declarations only
grow, so a skeleton that type-checked once still does, and a later
=label= on the same document replays nothing it has seen.
=typecheck expanded.= (or =FSP_TYPECHECK=expanded=) selects the old
whole-term replay, which is the reference the per-definition check is
tested against; both accept and reject the same documents.

*One statement, two spellings.* The graph identifies =~Q= with
=Q -> false= (=canonical_prop=, lesson 2); Fellowship does not. When one
argument needs =~Q= and another derives =Q -> false=, the unfolded term
holds a proof of =Q -> false= where =~Q= is required, and the replay is
refused when the scaffold throws back to its catch variable. No skeleton
contains that join - the site is open there - so the per-definition check
alone would accept a debate the whole-term replay refuses.
=SharedDebate.spelling_clashes()= detects a statement spelled in more
than one way among the arguments it reaches, and the CLI then replays the
expanded term instead. The refusal is an old one, recorded as
aida-negation-spelling-in-unfolding; the fallback keeps it as it was.

* Lesson 17 - Instances: the issue graph and the strict phase without the term

** Definition

An *instance* is a statement together with the statements captured and
cut in it - restricted to what the statement can reach - and it is the
unit the compiler and the strict phase work on. Two citations of the
same statement under the same captures and cuts, like the two in lesson
15, are one instance: the binders' names are parameters, written =§=
internally, not part of the key. Where nothing is captured, there is
exactly one instance per statement.

Why not one definition? Strictness is read off the concrete term (lesson
10). A sub-debate whose only site is captured is strict; the same
sub-debate with that site open is not. So the same definition is decided
differently under different captures, and what can be memoised is the
instance.

=IssueResolver= (=core/dc/instances.py=) does two things with
instances.

*Compile.* An instance's term is its definition's body with each
citation replaced by what the unfolder would put there: the capturing
variable, a bare site for a cut statement, or - for a cited instance - a
citation leaf that the compiler reads as a *source* of the cited
statement, of the kind the statement's own site has. Meeting that leaf,
the compiler first compiles the cited instance (a hook on the compiler's
citation branch), so the edges come out in the order the unfolded term
would give them, each once. The issue graph is the union.

*Resolve.* =strict_resolve= is unchanged. It runs on an instance's term
in which each cited instance is a *stub*: a chain of leaves holding
exactly what an enclosing scaffold may ask of the resolved instance - an
open site if one remains in it, the captured variables still free in it,
and whether a strict decision was made inside. The strict phase's own
predicates (open sites, free names, decisions) read a stub like any
subterm, so a scaffold is decided here exactly as it is on the unfolded
term. Cited instances are resolved first and memoised.

*Strict edges* are collected across stubs, as the collector of lesson 10
collects them: the outermost closed subterms that owe their closedness to
a decision. Only such a subterm is written out in full, because =adopt=
replays it. =fold_occurrences= then has nothing to fold: where the
unfolded term gave one edge per copy (a1_0 and a1_0_2), the instance
gives one.

* Lesson 18 - Evaluation without unfolding, and the two routes

** Definition

=evaluate_shared= (=core/comp/evaluate.py=) runs the pipeline of the
first sequel on the shared debate. Phase 1 is the per-instance strict
phase of lesson 17, already done when the issue graph was built. Phase 2
is =resolve_scaffolds=, walking top-down from the issue's resolved term
as before, with one addition: when the walk reaches a stub, it writes
the cited instance out one level - its resolved term, under the names
the captured variables have at that site, still holding stubs for what
it cites in turn. The walk only reaches a stub inside a wing it keeps.
A dropped wing is never written out at all.

To decide a scaffold, phase 2 compiles the scion (lesson 11). A stub
inside the scion is read as the compiler reads it: as a source of its
statement, when the resolved instance still has the statement's own site
as its innermost original; opened one level and read on, otherwise.

After phase 2 the sites are renumbered in reading order, since an
instance written out in two places brings the same sites twice, and
phase 3 normalises as before. What is normalised has the size of the
answer, not of the debate.

*The two routes.* =pipeline shared.= selects this route (the default
until 2026-10-06; see the note in "About this sequel"); =pipeline unfolded.=
(or =FSP_PIPELINE=unfolded=) makes =graph=, =label= and =evaluate= build
the unfolded term and run the first sequel's pipeline on it. The two
give the same graph, labels, normal form and class; what differs is the
names of binders and the numbers of sites, which the unfolded route takes
from the order of unfolding. On every statement of every fixture, in
both modes and under both base strategies, the two routes have been
compared and agree. If the shared route ever fails other than by a
refusal, the CLI warns and runs the unfolded one.

*Limits that remain.* The walks recurse: a chain of 90 arguments is
refused by =evaluate= and one of 150 by =label=, with one line
(aida-deep-term-recursion). The multi-extension semantics are as slow as
before: adf-bdd takes seconds for four independent even loops under
complete semantics (aida-multi-extension-labelling-cost). On small
debates the shared route is no faster than the unfolded one.

** Example

Lesson 14's document, =explain a2_0.= on the shared route, the sigma
stage:

: sigma          supporter P2[t] is IN -> keep the supporter, dropping the original and the 1 scaffold(s) inside it
: sigma          supporter P1[t] is IN -> keep the supporter, dropping the original and the 1 scaffold(s) inside it
: evaluate       'a2_0' - 0 scaffold(s) decided by strictness over 3 instance(s), 2 by sigma (skeptical tiebreak, base cbn)
: evaluate       the term after both phases, going into normalisation:
: evaluate         μa2_1:P2.<μth_2:P2.<r2_1:P1->P2||μa1_1:P1.<μth_4:P1.<r1_1:P0->P1||!u1:P0*th_4:P1>||a1_1:P1>*th_2:P2>||a2_1:P2>
: normalise      the normal form:
: normalise        μa2_1:P2.<r2_1:P1->P2||μb1:P1.<r1_1:P0->P1||!u1:P0*b1:P1>*a2_1:P2>

At P2 the outermost scaffold - a2_1's - is decided first and keeps its
supporter, which contains a stub for P1; the stub is opened, its
scaffolds decided, and a1_1's supporter kept. a2_0's support scaffold,
and the second copy of P1's debate it would have held, are never
written out. Under =pipeline unfolded.= the same two decisions are made on
the unfolded term, and the normal form is the same up to one site
number:

: normalise        μa2_1:P2.<r2_1:P1->P2||μb1:P1.<r1_1:P0->P1||!u11:P0*b1:P1>*a2_1:P2>

=u11= is the eleventh site the unfolder reached; =u1= is the first site
of the answer.

Sixteen levels (=bench/gen.py double 16=): =evaluate a16_0.= answers
VALUE in about a second, and the normal form is a chain of sixteen
arguments with one presumption site at the bottom - 65 nodes, on the
generated document of the test - where the unfolded term would have
about 1.8 million.

* What this means for the specification

debate-graph-spec.org describes unfolding as the bridge from the graph
to evaluation (the Layer 2->4 interface). That bridge is now the
reference route, and the production route computes the same things
without the term. Three notions deserve a place in the spec:

  - the *shared form*: one definition per statement, and citations with
    captures and cuts;
  - the *instance*: a statement with the statements captured and cut in
    it, the unit at which strictness is read off;
  - *lazy opening*: sigma writes a sub-debate out only inside a wing it
    keeps, so a lost sub-debate costs nothing.

Open after the sharing work (tasks.org): listing a document's statements
and arguments from the CLI, and naming an anonymous sub-debate
(aida-cli-inventory-and-naming); the two spellings of a negation
(aida-negation-spelling-in-unfolding); the recursion limits
(aida-deep-term-recursion); and the fallback to the unfolded route,
which should go once the shared route has seen real documents.
