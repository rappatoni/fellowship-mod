* About this course

Eight lessons on how AIDA handles quantifiers: from Fellowship's four
first-order steps, through statements under a binder, to the individuals
arguments talk about - generic ones, chosen ones, and the ones a pronoun
points back to. Each lesson has a *definition*, a *worked example* with
real output, and *exercises*. Solutions are in the appendix.

It assumes the first minicourse (minicourse.org: statements, sides,
hyperedges, labels) and lesson 8a of minicourse-evaluation.org
(unfolding a debate into one term). Words from the glossary
(glossary.org) are used as defined there; new ones are defined where
they first appear.

The fixtures are in =tests/discourse/= and =tests/minicourse/=. Every
first-order debate with referents needs the unfolded route, so each
fixture starts with =pipeline unfolded.=

* Lesson 1 - Individuals, sorts and the four steps

** Definition

A *sort* is a declared type of individuals, =declare Pub : type.= A
*constant* is a declared individual, =declare c : Pub.= A predicate takes
individuals, =declare Dr : Pub -> bool.=, and a term in a proposition is
written in brackets, =Dr [c]=, =Dr [x]=.

Fellowship has four rules for the quantifiers, one per quantifier and
side. Each leaves a mark in the proof term:

| step                   | tactic      | proof term   | what the argument does            |
|------------------------+-------------+--------------+-----------------------------------|
| universal, right       | =elim z=    | =λz:Pub.v=   | names an arbitrary individual =z= |
| existential, right     | =elim [c]=  | =(c, v)=     | names the witness =c=             |
| universal, left        | =elim [c]=  | =c * e=      | names the counterexample =c=      |
| existential, left      | =elim=      | =(x:Pub).e=  | names an arbitrary individual =x= |

On the left the proof term is a context, which refutes its proposition:
=c * e= refutes the universal by refuting its instance at =c=, and
=(x:Pub).e= refutes the existential by refuting its instance at an
arbitrary =x=. Read as using the left-side proposition as an assumption,
the same two steps instantiate the universal at =c= and unpack the
existential into a fresh =x=.

Two of them introduce a variable: the universal on the right and the
existential on the left. Such a variable names an individual nobody
chose - an arbitrary one, for which the proof or the refutation must go
through whichever individual it is. (Seen from whoever uses the
existential as an assumption, it is the individual the existential
promises, about which nothing else is known.) The course calls these
two the *binders*. The other two, the existential on the right and the
universal on the left, are *witness positions*: there the argument
itself says which individual it means.

** Example

=tests/minicourse/first_order_steps.fspy= has one argument per step.
The terms Fellowship builds (=Enriched proof term= in the log):

#+begin_example
everyone      μeveryone:forall z:Pub,Dr z.<λz:Pub.!1.1:Dr z||everyone:...>
someone       μsomeone:exists x:Pub,Dr x.<(c,!1.1:Dr c)||someone:...>
from_everyone ...μk:Dr c.<h:forall z:Pub,Dr z||c*1.1.2.1:Dr c?>...
from_someone  ...μk:Dr c.<h:exists x:Pub,Dr x||(x:Pub).1.1.2.1:Dr x?>...
#+end_example

=!1.1:Dr z= is a presumed site (=by default=), =1.1.2.1:Dr c?= an owed
site on the left (=next=): the open places of an argument, as in the
propositional fragment. What is new is what surrounds them. In
=everyone= the site =Dr z= sits under =λz=, so it is a claim about an
arbitrary individual. In =from_someone= the site =Dr x= sits under
=(x:Pub).=, so it is about an arbitrary individual: the refutation of
the existential must refute =Dr x= whichever individual =x= is.

2. Replace =elim [c].= in =someone= by =elim [!w].= and add the

   notation). What term comes out?

* Lesson 2 - Statements under a binder

** Definition

A site under a binder states something about the binder's variable, and
the variable means nothing outside the argument. AIDA therefore keeps
the variable's *context* with the statement: the names in scope at the
site, each with its sort. The statement in its context is written

: [y?:Pub] ⊢ Eff

and is a node of its own, different from the closed =Eff=. A name a
binder introduced is a *binder entry*; the mark after its name shows the
onus of the statement at that site, =y?= owed, =y!= presumed. It is
display only: an owed and a presumed statement are one node.

A closed statement bears on itself in every larger context. If =Eff=
holds, it holds for any =y=. AIDA adds a *weakening* edge from =Eff= to
=[y?:Pub] ⊢ Eff=, on both sides, so closed supporters and
counterarguments reach the site.

In the debate graph a first-order argument keeps its quantifier steps
together: the steps that carry the binder's variable are the *spine* of
the argument and are not split. The parts off the spine are *horns*
(glossary): each becomes a node of its own where another argument
engages its statement. One exception is still open: a horn whose
statement mentions a variable bound inside the argument is kept in
place (tasks.org, aida-referents-open-horns).

** Example

=tests/discourse/weakening.fspy=: =a= argues =forall y:Pub, Use= from the
rule =Eff -> Use= and leaves =Eff= owed under =y=. The closed =e= argues
=Eff= from a presumed =Rain=, the closed counterargument =c= argues
against it from a presumed =Strike=. =graph a= (abridged):

#+begin_example
forall y:Pub,Use[term]
`-- a  (supporter, defeasible)
    |-- Eff->Use[term]
    |   `-- r1  (subargument, strict)
    `-- [y?:Pub] ⊢ Eff[term]  [obligation]
        |-- weakening  (weakening, defeasible)
        |   `-- Eff[term]
        |       |-- e  (supporter, defeasible)
        ...
        `-- contested by [y?:Pub] ⊢ Eff[context]
            `-- weakening  (weakening, defeasible)
                `-- Eff[context]
                    |-- c  (supporter, defeasible)
#+end_example

=label a= gives =[y?:Pub] ⊢ Eff= UNDEC on both sides, and so
=forall y:Pub,Use= UNDEC: =e= and =c= rest on presumptions that oppose
each other, and the conflict reaches the site by weakening.
=evaluate a= is open, =?UNDEC:forall y:Pub,Use=.

** Exercises

1. Why is =a='s site shown =[y?:Pub] ⊢ Eff= and not =Eff=?
2. Would a closed argument for =Eff= alone, without =c=, make =a= a
   value?

* Lesson 3 - Discourse referents and generic arguments

** Definition

A *discourse referent* is a name an argument uses for an individual it
does not declare: =?x= or =!x=. Fellowship keeps it in the argument's
environment, never in the signature. An argument can list its referents
before its statement, =[?x:Pub]=, or have them collected from it.

The sigil before the name is the onus of the *individual*: =?x= owes an
individual, =!x= presumes one. It is separate from the onus of a
statement about it, which is how the argument leaves the site
(=by default= presumes, =next= owes). The two combine freely.

A referent the argument's conclusion mentions is a *parameter*. The
argument is then *generic*: it stands for one copy per individual, made
by substitution. Where it fills a site, AIDA matches its conclusion
against the site's statement *one-sidedly*: the generic argument's
parameters receive terms, the site's names stay fixed (core/dc/matching.py).
So =Dr ?x -> false= matches =Dr z -> false= with =?x := z=, and never the
other way round.

A parameter's sigil matters only where nothing gives it a value, that
is where the generic argument's own conclusion is the issue: =?x= owes a
witness and the term stays open; =!x= presumes the sort has an
individual (lesson 6) and the term can be a value.

** Example

=tests/discourse/peirce_fo.fspy=: =a= proves Peirce's law for every =z=
and leaves =Dr z -> false= owed under =z=. =sup : (Dr [?x] -> false)= is
generic and fills that site with =?x := z=. =graph a=:

#+begin_example
forall z:Pub,((Dr z->false)->Dr z)->Dr z[term]
|-- a  (supporter, defeasible)
|   `-- [?x:Pub] ⊢ Dr ?x->false[term]  [obligation]
|       `-- sup  (supporter, defeasible)
|           `-- [?x:Pub] ⊢ Dr ?x[context]  [presumption]
`-- a*  (strict, strict)
#+end_example

The site is shown as =sup= states it, =[?x:Pub] ⊢ Dr ?x->false= (one
statement, one name). =evaluate a= is a value; =sup= appears in it
instantiated:

#+begin_example
...μsup:Dr z->false.<λp:Dr z.μm:false.<p:Dr z||g:Dr z>||sup:Dr z->false>*g:Dr z>...
#+end_example

=sup='s presumed =Dr ?x= on the left became =Dr z= and was captured by
=a='s own =g=.

The parameter's sigil at the top: in =tests/discourse/counter_instance.fspy=
the generic =gen : P [?z]= labelled as its own issue gives
=a witness for ?z= OUT and =[?z:iota] ⊢ P ?z= OUT; =evaluate gen= is
open, =μx2:P ?z.<?OUT:P ?z||x2:P ?z>=.

** Exercises

1. Replace =?x= by =!x= in =sup=. Does =evaluate a= change?
2. Why can =sup= not fill a site =Dr c -> false= /and/ a site
   =Dr d -> false= with the same =?x=?

* Lesson 4 - Instances

** Definition

A generic statement *covers* every statement that is it with terms for
its parameters, by the same one-sided matching: =beat ?x ?y= covers
=beat peter conrad= and =beat conrad conrad=; =beat ?x ?x= does not cover
=beat conrad peter=. AIDA copies every argument deriving or contesting a
generic statement to each *instance* that is a node of the document,
with its sources instantiated (=DebateGraph.instantiate=). The default
an argument asserts of its own conclusion goes along; the onus a site
sets elsewhere does not. Influence runs downwards only: an instance's
fate never reaches the generic statement.

Instances form a lattice: =beat ?x conrad= lies between =beat ?x ?y= and
=beat peter conrad=. =graph= draws a dotted =instance= edge from each
generic statement to its *nearest* instances only.

** Example

=tests/discourse/conrad_peter.fspy=: =brawl= owes =beat conrad peter=
and the contrary of =beat peter conrad=; the generic =supp : beat [?x] [?y]=
presumes everybody beats everybody; the closed counterargument
=calm : beat [conrad] [peter]= presumes the contrary of one instance.
=label brawl=:

#+begin_example
beat peter conrad [term] IN
~beat peter conrad [context] IN
beat conrad peter [term] UNDEC
beat conrad peter [context] UNDEC
beat conrad peter-~beat peter conrad [term] UNDEC
#+end_example

=supp='s presumption reaches both instances; =calm= opposes it at
=beat conrad peter= only, so that instance is UNDEC and the other IN.

=tests/discourse/beat_lattice.fspy= adds =everyone_beats_conrad : beat [?x] [conrad]=,
=peter_beats_everyone : beat [peter] [?y]= and the counterargument
=nobody_beats_conrad : beat [?a] [conrad]=. =label fights= gives
=beat peter pedro= IN, while =beat peter conrad= and
=beat conrad conrad= are UNDEC: the counterargument covers exactly the
instances ending in =conrad=.

** Exercises

1. In beat_lattice, which arguments bear on =beat peter pedro=?
2. =calm= contests =beat conrad peter=. Does it reach
   =beat peter conrad=?

* Lesson 5 - Counter-instances and the closed universal

** Definition

A generic argument and a closed universal say different things. A
generic =gen : P [?z]= by default is a *default for each individual*: a
counter-instance =~P c= defeats the instance =P c= and nothing else. A
closed =forall x, P x= is *one statement*: it falls as a whole when a
counterargument instantiates it at an individual that fails
(tasks.org, aida-referents-counter-instances). AIDA makes no difference
between a universal introduced on the right and one used on the left.

** Example

=tests/discourse/counter_instance.fspy=: =gen : P [?z]= by default; =a=
argues =forall x, P x= leaving =P x= owed under =x= (filled by =gen=);
=not_c= refutes =P c= strictly from the denial =not_pc=; =no= argues
against the universal on the left, =[?c:iota] (forall x:iota, P [x]). elim [?c]. next.=
=label a=:

#+begin_example
forall x:iota,P x [term] OUT
forall x:iota,P x [context] IN
P c [term] OUT
P c [context] IN
[?z:iota] ⊢ P ?z [term] IN
#+end_example

=evaluate a= is an exception:
=μalt3:forall x:iota,P x.<?OUT:forall x:iota,P x||c*μ'b1:P c.<b1:P c||not_pc:P c>>=.
=no='s open choice found the individual =c= (lesson 6), where =not_c=
refutes =P c=, so the universal falls as one. The generic statement
=[?z:iota] ⊢ P ?z= stays IN: =gen= has lost only its instance at =c=.

** Exercises

1. Remove =no=. What does =evaluate a= give, and why does =not_c= not
   defeat =a= on its own?

* Lesson 6 - Open choices, the sort node and witness scaffolds

** Definition

A referent in an argument's environment that its conclusion does *not*
mention is the argument's *open choice*: an individual it names at a
witness position, =elim [?w]=, and nobody gives it from outside.
Statements about it are that argument's own: another argument's
statement with the same wording is about another individual. In keys
AIDA writes it =w#owner:name=.

Where the argument names its open choice, it rests on one more source:

- =?w= owes /a witness for ?w of A/, an individual it must still find;
- =!w= presumes the *sort node* =exists x:Pub, true=, that the sort has
  an individual. A declared constant proves the sort node strictly; a
  counterargument can contest it.

AIDA then tries individuals for the choice. At the quantified statement
it builds a *witness scaffold*: a support scaffold whose root is the
open choice itself and whose *alternatives* each read the argument's body
for one individual. The candidates, bottom of the stack first: declared
constants, the =!= choices of earlier arguments (lesson 7), and the
variables of binders in scope, the innermost on top. The scaffold keeps
the top alternative that stands; if none does, the root. A =?= choice is
never a candidate: a question supplies no individual.

For a =!= choice an alternative wins only where its statement is argued
of that individual elsewhere: its =by default= is about its own
individual, not about the candidate. So an indefinite stays new unless
somebody argues otherwise.

** Example

=tests/discourse/witness_import.fspy=: =a= argues
=forall y, Q y -> exists x, Q x=, introduces =y= and the hypothesis
=h : Q y=, and leaves the existential owed. =w : [?x:Pub] (exists x:Pub, Q [x]). elim [?x].=
fills it with its own choice. In =graph a=, =w= owes
=a witness for ?x of w= and =[?x:Pub] ⊢ Q ?x=. Under =a='s binder the
scaffold tries =y=, and =h= captures =Q y=. =evaluate a= is a value:

#+begin_example
μa:...<λy:Pub.λh:Q y.μw:exists x:Pub,Q x.<(y,h:Q y)||w:exists x:Pub,Q x>||a:...>
#+end_example

=tests/discourse/open_choice.fspy=: =some_donkey= chooses =?w= for
=exists y, donkey y=; =pd= argues =donkey [pedro]=. =evaluate some_donkey=:
=(pedro,!IN:donkey pedro)=. =rally='s own =?u= is never offered to
=some_donkey=.

=tests/discourse/sort_node.fspy=: =some_farmer : [!x:iota] (exists x:iota, farmer [x]). elim [!x]. by default.=
=label some_farmer= gives =exists x:iota,true= IN (presumed) and the
argument is a value, =(!x,!IN:farmer !x)=.

** Exercises

1. Remove =pd= from open_choice.fspy. What does =evaluate some_donkey=
   give?
2. Why does the scaffold for =w= in witness_import try =y= but not a
   constant?

* Lesson 7 - Indefinites and pronouns

** Definition

The two sigils of an open choice differ in where the argument's own
onus goes (decided 2026-10-22, tasks.org, aida-referents-anaphora):

- =!w=, an *indefinite* ("a knight walks"), presumes an individual of
  its own. Its =by default= is about that individual. A candidate wins
  only where the statement is argued of it elsewhere.
- =?w=, a *pronoun* ("he whistles"), owes the individual. Its
  =by default= is about whoever answers: each alternative's sites keep
  the onus the argument wrote.

The candidates are the same for both: constants, binder variables in
scope, and the =!= choices of arguments registered *before* the
choosing one, its *antecedents*, the latest on top. An argument with
parameters offers none of its choices, since its choice is one for all
its instances (tasks.org, aida-referents-choice-under-parameter, under
review). A borrowed individual rests on its sort node, as its owner's
choice does.

Because a scaffold keeps one alternative, a pronoun resolves to one
individual. A generic =whistles [!c]= would instead make every
individual whistle.

** Example

=tests/discourse/anaphora.fspy= ("A plumber walks. A knight walks. He
whistles."): two indefinites, each with its own =!a=, then
=he_whistles : [?c:iota](exists x:iota, whistles [x]). elim [?c]. by default.=
=label he_whistles=:

#+begin_example
exists x:iota,whistles x [term] IN
a witness for ?c of he_whistles [term] OUT
[!a:iota] ⊢ whistles !a [term] IN
[!a_2:iota] ⊢ whistles !a_2 [term] IN
#+end_example

=!a_2= is the knight's =!a=, renamed in the pronoun's term because =!a=
is taken there by the plumber's. Both readings stand; the scaffold keeps
the top one. =evaluate he_whistles=:

#+begin_example
μhe_whistles:exists x:iota,whistles x.<(!a_2,!IN:whistles !a_2)||he_whistles:...>
#+end_example

=tests/discourse/anaphora_silent.fspy= ("A queen walks. A knight walks
silently. He whistles."): the knight's argument presumes he does not
whistle, so the knight's reading meets opposing presumptions and is
UNDEC, and the scaffold falls back to the queen,
=(!a,!IN:whistles !a)=.

The same rule applies to any =?= choice. In sort_node.fspy
=some_farmer_owed= (=?x=) now resolves to =some_farmer='s farmer:
=(!x,!IN:farmer !x)=.

Not done yet: a resolution supports nothing outside the pronoun's own
argument, so no other argument can use "the knight whistles"
(tasks.org, aida-referents-anaphora-export).

** Exercises

1. Swap =plumber_walks= and =knight_walks= in anaphora.fspy. Which
   reading does =he_whistles= keep?
2. Write the pronoun with =!c= instead of =?c=. What does
   =evaluate he_whistles= give, and what does it mean?

* Lesson 8 - Labels, terms, and what is still open

** Definition

A first-order debate is labelled on the *issue graph*: the part of the
document graph its issue reaches, plus the edges AIDA reads off the
unfolded term for what the document does not hold - the copies of
generic arguments placed in it and the alternatives of witness
scaffolds (=strict.instance_edges=). Evaluation then judges the term
itself: an expression stands for what it is in the term, and labels
decide only what the term cannot (core/comp/standing.py). A normal form
that still mentions an open =?x= is no value; one that mentions only
=!x= can be.

Debates with referents, open choices or instances need
=pipeline unfolded.=; the shared route refuses them.

** Example

In witness_import.fspy, =label a= gives =a='s issue IN but the site
=[y?:Pub] ⊢ exists x:Pub,Q x= OUT. The document graph does not know
that =h= captures =Q y= inside =a='s term; the term does, so =evaluate a=
is a value (lesson 6). =evaluate w= alone is open,
=?OUT:exists x:Pub,Q x=: outside =a= nothing answers =w='s question.
