* Summary

1. *The type check slows down superlinearly with term size.* Five
   mechanisms, each measured:
   - Fellowship rebuilds the whole proof term on every tactic step.
   - Fellowship runs as OCaml bytecode.
   - The replay ends with one full snapshot that pretty-prints the whole
     term.
   - The wrapper reads that snapshot with pexpect in 2,000-byte chunks,
     re-searching the whole buffer after each.
   - Python parses the returned term with an Earley parser.

   Three fixes prototyped together (path copying in Fellowship, a native
   build, pexpect settings) take a 45,000-node replay from 331 s to 57 s.
   Fellowship's part goes from 280 s to 15 s and is now roughly linear;
   the Python parse-back (32 s) is the largest remaining cost.
2. *A single statement spelled two ways silently switches the type check
   to the whole expanded term.* =~A= and =A -> false= is enough. In the
   IRIS combined document that meant 62,000 nodes instead of 1,800 and
   about 25 minutes instead of 2 s.
3. *Preferred (the default semantics) and plain stable are
   generate-and-test in adf-bdd.* They enumerate the 3^u resp. 2^u
   interpretations above the grounded model, whatever the BDD backend.
   adf-bdd's nogood-learning stable routine is about 150x faster, but
   only with =--lib hybrid=; with =naive= it silently prints nothing.
   With the grounded labelling solved first and only the undecided rest
   given to =hybrid --stmng=:
   - it agrees with =naive --stm= on all 870 distinct graphs of the test
     suite
   - it removes hybrid's cost on long chains
4. *Upstream:* adf-bdd has had no performance work since v0.3.0 (2022).
   Newer solvers exist (BAss, 2026; k++ADF).

* 1. Labelling: adf-bdd

** How AIDA calls it

=core/comp/oracle.py:385=:
=adf-bdd -q --lx --lib naive {--grd|--com|--stm} FILE=, timeout 120 s.
- Preferred = the ≤_i-maximal members of =--com=, filtered in Python
  (=core/comp/adf_label.py:363=).
- =--lib naive= was chosen on 2026-09-16: hybrid grew about quartically
  on support chains.

** What adf-bdd does (source, adf_bdd 0.3.1 and adf-bdd-bin 0.3.0)

- =complete()= (=adf.rs:678=) iterates *every* three-valued
  interpretation extending the grounded model
  (=ThreeValuedInterpretationsIterator=) and keeps the complete ones.
  That is generate-and-test over 3^u candidates, u = statements left
  undecided by grounded.
- =stable()= (=adf.rs:264=) does the same over the 2^u two-valued
  extensions.
- =stable_nogood= (=--stmng=) and =two_val_nogood= (=--twoval=) search
  with learned nogoods. Further stable variants: =--stmca=, =--stmcb=
  (model counting), =--stmpre=, =--stmrew=, =--stmrew2=.
- =main.rs=: only the =hybrid= branch dispatches the nogood and
  counting modes. Under =--lib naive= the binary handles only =--grd=,
  =--com= and =--stm=, and *silently ignores* every other mode flag:
  exit 0, no output.
- The library README: =naive= is adf-bdd's own OBDD implementation. Its
  formula-to-BDD construction "does not perform well on bigger formulae",
  but it offers reuse of reductions, memoisation and counter-model
  counting. =biodivine= (biodivine-lib-bdd) builds BDDs fast. =hybrid=
  instantiates with biodivine and solves on the naive representation
  (=Adf::from_biodivine=).

** Grounded reduction (prototype in =scripts/adf_routes.py=)

Every stable model extends the grounded model. So:
1. Compute grounded with =naive --grd=.
2. Fix the decided statements and simplify the acceptance conditions.
3. Give only the undecided rest to =hybrid --stmng=.
4. Add the fixed values back.

Same model sets as =naive --stm= on:
- chains of 100, 400 and 800 (all decided by grounded: no stable search)
- layered graphs of 213 and 858
- 4 even loops
- the IRIS graph: 0.01 s instead of 8.8 s

That the reduction preserves stable models for ADFs in general needs a
proof; it is the analogue of well-founded simplification for normal
logic programs. The agreement is evidence, not proof.

  [[https://github.com/ellmau/adf-obdd/commits/main][Commits on main]]

- *k++ADF* ([[https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/group/coreo/k++adf/][Helsinki]],
  SAT-based, MIT): credulous (=-c=) and skeptical (=-s=) acceptance
  without enumeration, and enumeration. The page lists
  cf/nai/adm/com/prf/grd, not stable.
- Not measured: encoding AIDA's ADFs for clingo, whose brave and
  cautious reasoning answers credulous and skeptical queries without
  enumeration.

* 2. Type check: the Fellowship replay

** What the type check does

=core/dc/typecheck.py=:
1. It turns the term into Fellowship tactics
   (=core/ac/instructions.py=).
2. It replays them in a throwaway theorem, in quiet machine mode
   (=wrap/prover.py:365=, =send_commands_quiet_final=).
3. It switches quiet mode off to receive the full state with the rebuilt
   proof term.
4. It parses that term (=core/ac/syntax.py=, Lark Earley).
5. It compares shapes.

On the shared route it replays one skeleton per sub-debate. It replays
the whole expanded term only if =typecheck expanded= is set or a
statement has two spellings (=wrap/service.py:943-947=).

** The mechanisms

1. *Whole-term rebuild on every step.*
   - =instantiate_in_t3rm= (=wrap/fellowship/core.ml:657-713=) fills one
     metavariable, but walks the whole proof term and allocates a new
     copy of every node, with no early exit and no sharing.
   - The tactic driver (=tactics.ml:227-241=) does list work over all
     open goals (=split_list=, =g_before @ l @ g_after=) and adds the
     goal's environment to the signature. In a debate, delegations and
     obligations stay open as goals: 253 at k = 6, 1,021 at k = 8.
   - Every successful tactic pushes the state onto the trace and onto
     =history= (=tactics.ml:802-804=).
   - Round trips with constant reply size (105 chars) grow from 302 to
     636 µs between the start and the middle of the k = 8 replay.
2. *Bytecode.* =OCamlMakefile='s default target is =byte-code=, so
   =fsp= runs under =ocamlrun=. =make nc= builds native code.
3. *The final full snapshot.*
   - =machine quiet off.= returns the full state (=machine.ml:110-157=):
     the whole term pretty-printed, its MD5, goals and declarations. At
     k = 8 that is 8.2 MB.
   - The printer builds strings by nested =sprintf= (=core.ml:601= ff.),
     copying each subterm once per enclosing level.
   - =discard theorem.= returns another full snapshot (272 KB).
4. *pexpect reads quadratically.* =PopenSpawn= defaults: =maxread=
   2,000, =searchwindowsize= None. After every 2,000-byte chunk the
   whole accumulated buffer is searched for the end marker. The 8.2 MB
   reply took 17 s.
5. *Earley parse-back.* It is roughly linear at first, then grows
   faster: 10 s at k = 8, 32 s at k = 9 (with the fixes below in place).

macOS =sample= of =fsp= during a long replay: most samples in =read=
(waiting for the wrapper), and the rest in =caml_bytecode_interpreter=.
Fellowship's resident memory could not be sampled reliably, so the role
of the retained history in the jump at k = 9 is inferred, not measured.

  equals the sent term up to alpha, site numbering and spelling.
